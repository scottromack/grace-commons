# GRACE lang — history

The dated record of how [GRACE lang](./GRACE-lang.md) got here, moved out of the grammar because a reader does not need it to use the grammar today: the Watch list's history on 2026-10-22, and the Changes section, Section 23, on 2026-10-23 at v0.66. The grammar keeps its rules and its live Watch list, and its Section 23 points here; everything smaller is in `git log -- GRACE-lang.md`.

In a rule an arithmetic expression is two operands joined by one of `+`, `-` and `*` inside one pair of parentheses (Term arithmetic expression, Sugar 11, Hard invariant 24); in a declaration, declared arithmetic sets every operator inside a pair of its own, a single one included, and pairs may nest (Term declared arithmetic, Closed vocabulary 31, Hard invariant 35); a division appears only in a declaration, which names its rounding — rounded down, rounded up, or rounded to nearest with ties to even (Term rounding, Closed vocabulary 32 and 33, Hard invariant 36 and 37); no operand is a negative literal (Closed vocabulary 34); the parser reads `×` as `*` (Sugar 12); Term arithmetic operator loses its precedence sentence, and Closed vocabulary 9 names a term for anything beyond one arithmetic expression. The Execution Contract states once that the arithmetic is exact (Logic confinement 9), which Cart's Capability requirement 4 had stated for one atom. Ruled by the maintainer on 2026-10-26 at 18:42Z after Grok's review, closing the loophole of a long formula wrapped in one pair of parentheses; the arithmetic sweep wrote 105 formulas on 98 declarations in 22 specifications in the new form, each grouped as the retired precedence read it and so to the same value, and this document's two examples with them.
