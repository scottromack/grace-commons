## FOUNDATIONAL FINDINGS

### F1 — `service_identity = none` mandates the exact state Generation acceptance check 2 fails

**WHERE:** [Open] step 3 (the "older-than arm does not fire" clause) vs. Generation acceptance check 2 (second key) vs. §*Which closing stands* vs. [Reconcile] step 5's duplicate scan.

**DEFECT:** [Open] step 3 instructs a `service_identity = none` deployment to *proceed past* an open intent older than the bound, leaving the old intent open and writing a second one — "the old intent stays open, surfaced on `compliance_surface` until an operator closes it through [Resolve]". Generation acceptance check 2's second key says "**at most one intent per `(kind, act_key)` is open at any reading inside the horizon** … Two of either is a conformance failure (`binding_duplicate`)", and it carries **no** `service_identity = none` branch (check 3 has one; check 2 does not). §*Which closing stands* independently declares "two *open intents* on one `(kind, act_key)` are also a `binding_duplicate`", and [Reconcile] step 5's scan is instructed to surface every such key. So a report-only deployment that follows the protocol exactly produces, by design and durably, the state three surfaces are built to report as a conformance failure — and the reports are indistinguishable from the fenceless-deployment duplicate the second key was added to catch (§*Composes*, `journal_fence`), which destroys the signal the second key exists to carry.

**FAILURE:** Kind `K` bound `service_identity = none`, `commit_fence = none`. Invocation A opens intent `I_A` at t and crashes. The sweep writes nothing for K. At t + 90 s a second caller invokes: [Open] takes the section, the pre-check finds `I_A` older than `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance`, the older-than arm does not fire, and [Open] writes `I_B`. Now `(K, act_key)` carries two open intents inside the horizon, indefinitely (until an operator's [Resolve]). [Reconcile] step 5 surfaces `binding_duplicate` on that key every run; [Read Invocation] sets `binding_duplicate`; and an auditor running check 2 fails the deployment. Nothing in the spec exempts any of the three.

**FIX:** Give check 2's second key, §*Which closing stands*'s second-key clause, and [Reconcile] step 5's open-intent scan the same `service_identity = none` branch check 3 carries — under a report-only binding, several open intents on one `(kind, act_key)` are the declared degradation, not a duplicate — and state the surfaced-not-duplicate distinction once, at the second key, rather than in each of the three.

### F2 — The walkthrough's `closure_latency` breaches the page's own disclosure rule, and the rule has no start-check landing

**WHERE:** Configuration §`closure_latency`; Examples §*Walkthrough — a disclosure, three ways* ("All four start conditions, shown … The instance starts"); Generation acceptance check 4.

**DEFECT:** Configuration §`closure_latency` states normatively: "`closure_latency` must exceed `read_bound + 2 × journal_write_bound + the probe's round-trip`". The walkthrough binds `read_bound = 2 s`, `journal_write_bound = 3 s`, `closure_latency = 5 s`. The required floor is `2 + 2×3 + probe = 8 s + probe`; the walkthrough discloses **5 s**. The instance the page presents as conforming ("The instance starts") violates a rule the page calls a disclosure "contradicting the operations it is disclosed over". Compounding it, that rule is **not** one of the "**four** conditions of instance start" Generation acceptance check 4 enumerates, so no check catches it in any deployment — the page's only defence against it is the sentence in Configuration.

**FAILURE:** Take the walkthrough's own crash scenario at the disclosed worst case. The sweep's lease is `closure_latency + journal_write_bound = 8 s`. [Reconcile] step 2 takes the section (remaining 8), performs the under-section re-read (`read_bound` 2 s → remaining 6), probes (round-trip p → remaining 6 − p), passes the `remaining ≥ journal_write_bound` gate and writes `ledger.recovery_intended` (3 s → remaining 3 − p), then re-tests the gate for the outcome write: `3 − p ≥ 3` is false for any p > 0. The run writes nothing further, counts the act in `skipped`, and the walkthrough's claim that "the disclosure is accounted for within about forty seconds … inside the ten-minute window" is false at the bounds the same paragraph discloses. Every subsequent run repeats the same failure, so the act is never closed by the sweep at all.

**FIX:** Raise the walkthrough's `closure_latency` above `read_bound + 2 × journal_write_bound + probe` (e.g. `closure_latency = 10 s`, `run_bound` re-derived) and re-show the four conditions; and add `closure_latency > read_bound + 2 × journal_write_bound + the probe's round-trip` to the instance-start conditions checked at start and to Generation acceptance check 4, which then names **five** conditions.

### F3 — Configuration and Bindings give `commit_fence` two different instants, and one of them violates Lease Invariant 6

**WHERE:** *Bindings* §`commit`/`commit_fence`; Configuration §`clock_skew_allowance`; *Primitive policies* §`now`.

**DEFECT:** Three passages state the fence instant three ways, and two of them cannot both be normative.
- *Bindings*: "its clock is the atom's … so the instant is minted with `clock_skew_allowance` already subtracted (Lease Invariant 6) **and** the sweep's abandon edge is the examine edge plus the allowance once more."
- Configuration §`clock_skew_allowance`: "`commit_fence` … is **not** the second: it carries the section's **bare** `expires_at` to the store and the allowance is spent instead on widening the sweep's abandon edge by one, **which is where *Bindings* puts it**." — an assertion about *Bindings* that *Bindings* contradicts.
- *Primitive policies* §`now`: "No write on this page is decided by a cross-seam comparison except at a declared fence, **`commit_fence` and `journal_fence`**, where the allowance is minted into the instant rather than applied at the reading (*Configuration*, `clock_skew_allowance`)" — citing, for the minted reading, the one entry that denies it.

`commit_fence` is by the page's own definition an `expires_at` handed to a store that judges it on another clock, so Lease Invariant 6 — "**every** such instant is minted with the cross-seam allowance already subtracted … independently" — applies to it. The Configuration entry's "bare `expires_at`" therefore attributes to the atom a semantics the atom does not have.

**FAILURE:** Two generators reading the page produce two different stores' deadlines. Generator A (Configuration) passes `expires_at`; generator B (Bindings, Primitive policies) passes `expires_at − clock_skew_allowance`. Under B, a conforming invocation that issues its commit in the last `clock_skew_allowance` of a lease that start check 3 sized to fit it exactly is fenced out, returns `unknown`, calls [Yield], and hands a perfectly healthy act to the sweep — a spurious `escalated`/`abandoned` on every act that runs near the bound. Under A, the fence is a bare instant judged on a second clock, which is precisely the construction Lease §*The terminus, and carrying it as a fence* declares non-conforming.

**FIX:** Delete the "bare `expires_at`" clause from Configuration §`clock_skew_allowance`; state once, in *Bindings*, that `commit_fence`'s instant is minted `expires_at − clock_skew_allowance` per Lease Invariant 6 **and** that the sweep's abandon edge is widened by a second allowance for the substrate-stamp comparison, and have Configuration and *Primitive policies* cite that sentence rather than paraphrase it.

### F4 — [Read Invocation] is required to report a duplicate its signature has nowhere to put

**WHERE:** `read_invocation` signature block; §*Composes*, `journal_fence` ("The surfaces are therefore stated over **both** keys"); §*Which closing stands* (second key).

**DEFECT:** Two passages require [Read Invocation] to report the second-key duplicate — two open intents on one `(kind, act_key)` — **on the act and explicitly not on an entry**: "[Read Invocation] reports the second on the act rather than on an entry" (§*Composes*) and "reported on the act rather than on an entry, since neither intent is wrong on its own and the fault is that both exist" (§*Which closing stands*). The signature is `{invocations: sequence of {invocation_id, state, binding_duplicate, records, more}, more}`: `binding_duplicate` exists only **inside** an invocation entry, and the top level carries only `invocations` and `more`. There is no act-level field. The page's own argument for adding the second key — "without the second key the commonest duplicate a fenceless deployment produces is invisible to all three [surfaces]" — therefore fails at one of the three it names.

**FAILURE:** A fenceless deployment appends a second live intent for one `act_key` (the state §*Composes* says is reachable under `journal_fence = none`). An adopter calls `read_invocation(kind, act_key)`. Both entries are returned; each is a well-formed single invocation with one intent and no duplicate closing, so a generator setting the per-entry field from §*Which closing stands* sets `binding_duplicate = false` on both, and the read reports a clean act. [Reconcile] step 5 and check 2 see the duplicate; the read that the adopter's re-entry arm and the auditor actually call does not.

**FIX:** Add an act-level `binding_duplicate: true | false` to the top level of `read_invocation`'s return (beside `invocations` and `more`), and say in the prose which key each of the two fields answers for — the entry-level field for two unsuperseded closings on one `invocation_id`, the act-level field for two open intents on one `(kind, act_key)`.

### F5 — [Read Invocation] has no landing for the range read's declared `unavailable` arm, so an outage must be answered as absence

**WHERE:** §*Composes*, *Instance capability requirements* ("`unavailable` is the arm every read path on this page transcribes — [Open] step 3's `journal-unavailable`, [Reconcile]'s of the same name, and the position arms at [Close], [Refuse] and [Resolve]"); `read_invocation` signature; Terms §*Journal Unavailable*.

**DEFECT:** The page declares `unavailable` as an arm of its own instance capability requirement and then enumerates its transcription sites — five of them. [Read Invocation] performs the same rebuild through the same range read ("Reads the act's invocations … through the same rebuild") and is not on the list. Its signature admits exactly one rejection, `not-known`, whose declared meaning is **absence** ("`not-known` … is an `act_key` with no readable intent at all"; Terms: "It is **absence**, not destruction"). The Journal Unavailable card's reasoning for the omission — "[Close], [Refuse] and [Resolve] do not carry it: by the time they read, the act's existence is already decided, so their read failures land on their own position's `recording-failure` arm" — does not reach [Read Invocation], which decides nothing and has no `recording-failure` arm; and the same card asserts the code covers "both positions that have one", of which there are three.

**FAILURE:** The journal is unreachable. An adopter's re-entry arm calls `read_invocation(kind, act_key)` to answer *is my act already done?*. The rebuild's range read answers `unavailable`. The signature offers no code for it, so the cheapest conforming implementation returns `not-known` — telling the adopter the act was never opened, for a key whose intent and outcome are both sitting in the journal. The page forbids exactly this substitution elsewhere (§*Lawful destruction is answered before absence*, and the `not-known` card's own insistence that absence and destruction are different answers) and lets it stand here for an outage.

**FIX:** Add `journal-unavailable` to `read_invocation`'s rejection set and to the Journal Unavailable card's list of positions (three, not two), and add [Read Invocation] to the enumeration of read paths that transcribe the requirement's `unavailable` arm.

### F6 — `findings` is declared a composition-owned middle, with no Contract classification and no named atom

**WHERE:** *Composition state* §`findings` and the closing sentence of *Composition state*.

**DEFECT:** Every other element on the page carries a Contract classification per `execution-contract.md` §Composition state — the two admitted values are *derived index* and *extraction-pending*, and *extraction-pending* obliges the spec to name the atom that will own the truth (Audit Trail discharges exactly this for `purged_events`, `event_to_attestation`'s purged half, and `erasure_outcomes`, each naming **Erasure Tombstone** *(forthcoming)*). `findings` is declared truth-bearing and non-derivable — "It is **not** a derived index and no rebuild reproduces it" — with a durability floor, a dedup key per shape, a `first_seen`/`last_seen` span with a lifecycle, and a per-`act_key` order. That is state carrying truth no constituent replays: the *extraction-pending* case exactly. The page instead repudiates the classification scheme ("An earlier round … closed the section with *there is no composition-owned middle*, which was false the moment the surface acquired a contract") and supplies **no** classification and **no** forthcoming atom in its place. Run against the composition-layer extraction gate, the candidate clears all three: Gate 1 — a compliance-finding register with spans recurs as an independently forced invariant (Audit Trail's own alerting obligation and its `audit.reconciliation`/`audit.compensation` closure markers are the same must-be-true reached from the other side); Gate 2 — no constituent owns it, and Audit Trail explicitly disclaims it ("alerted on by the deployment"); Gate 3, the decisive one — it **introduces state the constituents do not carry**, a condition record with its own open/advancing/ceased lifecycle. That is a freestanding concept, not wiring.

**FAILURE:** An implementer reads the four classified elements, learns that a lost derived-index entry is "a rebuild trigger, not data loss", reaches `findings`, finds no classification, and holds it in process memory — losing exactly the outage span the page then requires Generation acceptance check 3 to clear on ("the exemption is keyed on an **instance finding's span**"). The page names this hazard verbatim and then leaves the classification that would foreclose it unstated. A second implementer, reading the same paragraph, treats `findings` as within the actions' atomicity surface, and a `compliance_surface` write failure now rejects a [Reconcile] run that has already landed closings.

**FIX:** Classify `findings` as **extraction-pending** and name its atom — a Compliance Finding / Alert Register *(forthcoming)* — restating the three Contract obligations for it as Audit Trail does for `erasure_outcomes` (durability obligation, no rebuild, flagged debt), and delete the "there is no composition-owned middle is false" sentence in favour of the classification.

## REFINING FINDINGS

- **R1** — Configuration §`run_bound` · the headroom floor `run_bound ≥ 2 × closure_latency + journal_write_bound` is derived from "a run that waits out **one dead holder** and then closes one act", but the same entry defines the wait as "one holder's remaining lease per act", and an invocation holder's lease is `completion_bound`, which start check 3 forces above `2 × read_bound + 2 × journal_write_bound + commit` and which the walkthrough itself charges at 30 s against a floor of 13 s → state the floor as `run_bound ≥ max(completion_bound, closure_latency + journal_write_bound) + closure_latency`.
- **R2** — Configuration §`closure_latency` / Generation acceptance check 4 · `closure_latency`'s must-exceed rule is stated normatively but is not among the "four conditions of instance start", so no start check and no acceptance check enforces it → add it as a fifth start condition and to check 4 (see F2).
- **R3** — Configuration §`compliance_surface`, *Composition state* §`findings`, check 3 · `store-unavailable` is an **instance** finding deduplicated on `finding` alone and carrying no `kind`, but a store is per-act-kind: one adopter's store outage blanket-exempts every other kind's intents from bounded closure → give `store-unavailable` a `kind` field and dedup it on `(finding, kind)`, with check 3's overlap test keyed on the kind's span.
- **R4** — *Composition state* §`findings` · `first_seen` "never moves" and "a condition ceases when a run does not [see it]", but nothing says whether a recurrence reopens the same record or starts a new one; check 3's exemption is an overlap test against the span, so a single record spanning two outages exempts every intent in the healthy gap between them → declare that a ceased condition closes its span and a recurrence opens a new record with a fresh `first_seen`.
- **R5** — [Reconcile] step 5 / check 3's `service_identity = none` branch · the at-risk surfacing has no declared `finding` value, so its act-finding dedup key `(invocation_id, finding)` is undefined and the check has no name to read for → name it (`closure-at-risk`) beside `journal-unavailable`, `store-unavailable` and `binding_duplicate`.
- **R6** — [Reconcile] step 4 vs. *Composition state* §`act_closings` · the state entry says `act_closings` is "Populated by [Close], [Refuse], [Resolve] and the sweep", while step 4 updates only `open_invocations` and `closed_acts` → add `act_closings` to step 4.

- **R10** — [Close] / check 2 shape (a) · nothing obliges [Close]'s `actor_ref` to be the same value [Open] was given, but check 2 fails an outcome carrying no `recovery` whose `actor_ref` differs from the intent's → state the obligation in [Close]'s preamble beside "the adopter's action's own".
- **R11** — §*Composes* (`read_record`), [Resolve]'s purge check, checks 1 and 5 · Audit Trail's projected `read_record(event_id) → audit_record | not-known` carries **no** failure arm ("that is why `read_record` carries a `not-known` arm and no rejection arm at all"), and this page declares outage arms only for the range read, so a journal outage at [Resolve]'s purge check has no landing and lands on `not-known` — absence for an outage → declare `unavailable` on `read_record` as part of the instance capability requirement, as the page already does for the range read, and map it to `recording-failure(resolution)` at [Resolve].
