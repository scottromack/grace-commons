F3 — The `completion_bound` start check undercounts by one journal write, so it passes on tuples where [Close] yields at its first step on every invocation
  WHERE: `### Configuration`, `journal_write_bound`; Generation acceptance check 4, third condition
  DEFECT: Both places state the check as `completion_bound > journal_write_bound + the commit's round-trip`, justified as "or [Close] would yield at its first step on every invocation". F4 — `max(completion_bound, closure_latency) > closure_latency + journal_write_bound` is unsatisfiable whenever `closure_latency ≥ completion_bound`, so the `max` is dead and a slow-closure deployment cannot start
  WHERE: `### Configuration`, `journal_write_bound` ("The sweep's lease carries the same check"); Generation acceptance check 4; `act_section` and [Reconcile] step 2 and [Resolve], which all take the sweep lease as `max(completion_bound, closure_latency)`
  DEFECT: If `closure_latency ≥ completion_bound` then `max(completion_bound, closure_latency) = closure_latency`, and the condition reduces to `closure_latency > closure_latency + journal_write_bound`, i.e. `0 > journal_write_bound` — false for every positive bound. So the check is satisfiable only when `completion_bound > closure_latency + journal_write_bound`, which forces `completion_bound > closure_latency` and makes `max(completion_bound, closure_latency)` identically `completion_bound` on every conforming instance. The `max` was introduced expressly "so that a slow closure is never cut off by a fast act's bound"; the start check makes that case unstartable rather than protected.
  FAILURE: A fast in-memory constituent with a cross-region `probe`: `completion_bound = 5 s` (honestly the longest a conforming invocation takes), `closure_latency = 20 s` (re-read + probe round-trip + two records), `journal_write_bound = 3 s`. Check 4 evaluates `max(5, 20) = 20 > 20 + 3 = 23` → false; the instance refuses to start. There is no legal repair: `completion_bound` is defined as the act's own longest conforming duration, so raising it to 24 s is a false declaration, and raising it also pushes the sweep's examine edge and the window inequality's first term out by 19 s.
  FIX: Replace the condition with `completion_bound > closure_latency + journal_write_bound` only where the sweep's lease is `completion_bound`, and state the sweep-lease condition on its own terms — the sweep's lease must exceed `closure_latency + journal_write_bound`, i.e. `max(completion_bound, closure_latency) ≥ closure_latency + journal_write_bound` is met by defining the sweep's lease as `closure_latency + journal_write_bound` rather than as a `max` of two unrelated quantities.

F5 — [Resolve]'s disposition table produces supersession chains; the exemptions that make them lawful are stated over pairs, and the page separately declares chains impossible
  WHERE: `#### resolve`, "the disposition against what already stands, in full" and "An escalation may be superseded once"; Generation acceptance check 2's exemption; `#### read_invocation`'s precedence and `binding_duplicate` rules
  DEFECT: The table permits any disposition over an `escalated` record and an `outcome` over an `abandoned` record. Composing those two permissions yields a three-record chain. A chain is not a pair, and the middle record belongs to two pairs, so a pairwise count leaves two standing closings. The page also asserts the opposite of its own table: "**An escalation may be superseded once**: a resolution is a closing and a second resolution would be closing over a closing, so re-escalation is not a disposition this action offers" — while the disposition list offered three sentences earlier is `outcome(...)`, `abandoned(cause)`, or `escalated(candidates)`.
  FAILURE: `commit_fence = none` (the page's declared common case). Sweep probes `not-committed` → writes E (`<kind>.escalated`, `cause = not-observed`). Check 2 collapses (E,A) and (A,O) as pairs, double-counting A, and reads two standing closings → conformance failure `binding_duplicate` on the page's own headline correction path. [Read Invocation] finds no pair and reports `binding_duplicate = true` for a lawfully corrected act.
  FIX: State supersession as transitive — the standing closing is the one no other closing names in `supersedes`, and every record reachable from it through `supersedes` counts with it as one closing — and reconcile the "superseded once" sentence with the disposition table by either removing `escalated` from the disposition list or deleting that sentence.

F8 — [Resolve]'s `invalid-request` payload does not cover three of the conditions the prose lands on it, and they imply different operator actions
  WHERE: `#### resolve` signature block, `invalid-request(purged | malformed)`; the prose's landings for the too-young abandonment, the over-cap candidate list, and `abandoned` over `abandoned`
  DEFECT: The signature declares exactly two payload members.
