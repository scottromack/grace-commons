## Summary

Recoverable Invocation is the protocol every regulated action in this library follows when it changes something it cannot take back — it moves custody, revokes a credential, discloses a record, closes a party — and must also leave a trustworthy account of having done so. The protocol has a shape that twenty compositions had each written out in their own words: write down what you are about to do, do it, write down what you did, and if the process dies in between, let a separate sweep finish the writing — carefully, so that the sweep and the original never both write, so that the sweep never writes about work that is still in progress, and so that it never invents an outcome it cannot read back from the store the act was made in.

This composition is that protocol, specified once. A pattern that adopts it no longer describes the protocol; it names this composition and supplies its **bindings** — what its act is, which store commits it, how long the act may take, and under what identity a sweep may finish the accounting. The intent record, the outcome record, the critical section that keeps two writers off one act, the sweep with its two edges, the position on every rejection code that tells a caller whether a retry is safe, and the arithmetic that makes the closure promise meetable all live here.

They are proved here too, in the formal model `recoverable-invocation.tla` (TLA+ — Temporal Logic of Actions, the library's behavioural-model notation; derived, holding, named under this page's Ledger): **six** components over one act — the invocation, the section host, the store, the journal, the sweep carried as two runs on two nodes, and an operator through [Resolve] as the third writer. Two of the six are writers the composition does not control, which is what the one-writer invariant is proved against.

Three things about the model are what make it worth having. Time is explicit. The crash can fall anywhere. And every write is split into the moment its gate is passed and the moment it is issued, so that a pause between the two is a behaviour the model can exhibit rather than one it defines away. The records-alone checks under Generation acceptance are the same surface on a running instance.

The composition wires two things: the **Audit Trail** substrate — the shared, regulated audit journal every pattern in this library records into, so that the intent and outcome records are attributed, sealed, and retained — and a **critical section** the deployment supplies keyed by the act (a lock: while one invocation holds it, no other invocation and no sweep may write for that act). It composes no store of its own: the act's store belongs to the adopter's constituent, and this composition reads it only through a read the adopter binds. Its emergent guarantee — the one no constituent provides alone — is that every act committed through it is accounted for exactly once, by exactly one writer, within a declared window or with a declared record of why not.

## Intent

Every composition in this library that commits an irreversible act against a constituent store and records that act in an audit journal faces the same three-write problem. The act cannot be withdrawn once the constituent commits it. The journal's append cannot be withdrawn once it lands. And no transaction spans the two, because neither constituent offers one — the library's rule since 2026-08-27 is that *an atomic set may not contain a write the host cannot take back* (§*Durability boundaries* in the methodology). So the writes are **ordered**, the order leaves exactly one reachable partial — the act committed, its record owed — and something must close that partial after the process that opened it is dead.

By 2026-08-30 the library's regulated compositions had each solved this in their own words — most of them the exact instance this page is the compound of, a few in a variant one binding away — and the two days of fresh-reader gates that ended that campaign found the same defects in every one of them: two writers landing an outcome for one act; a sweep reading work still in flight as a crash; a sweep re-emitting an outcome it could not have known; a rejection code that told the caller nothing had committed when something had; a closure window whose arithmetic could not be met. Each pattern's fix of one of these was where the next round's finding lived. That is not twenty bugs. It is one protocol carried in many places where it should be carried in one; the evidence that it clears the methodology's *higher-order composition test* is set out under the load-bearing wiring decision below.

This composition is that compound. It owns the protocol and its invariants; an adopter owns its act. The emergent rule it enforces: **one act, one intent, one outcome, one writer** — the invocation while it lives and holds the act's section, the sweep after the invocation has yielded, never both — with the sweep bounded below by the act's completion bound and above by the journal's retention horizon, writing under a declared service identity behind its own intent record, and writing only what the adopter's store can re-derive.

What this composition is *not*: it is not a transaction, and it claims none; it is not the adopter's store, and it never writes to one; it is not an audit journal, which is Audit Trail's; and it is not a class an adopter inherits from. An adopter names it in *Composes* the way it names an atom, binds its parameters, declares any deviation from the common contract, and deletes the paragraphs this page replaces. The constituent atoms are unchanged.

## Composes

- **[Lease](../atoms/lease.md)** — the per-key grant of exclusive standing whose terminus is an instant. This composition's act section **is** a lease, and so are both of its fences: a fence is the lease's `expires_at` handed to a party that will judge it on another clock, which the atom specifies once. Everything below binds the atom's parameters — which key, which holder, how long, which of its arms maps to which code of this page — and restates none of its semantics. What this page adds and the atom refuses to own is *which* key protects *which* work and *how long* a grant must last, both of which are this protocol's business.
- **[Audit Trail](./audit-trail.md)** — the regulated-audit **substrate** and this composition's journal, named the way an atom is named, its four constituents (Event Log, Actor Identity, Tamper Evidence, Retention Window) reached transitively per the *Compositions of compositions* convention ([`spec-format.md`](../spec-format.md)). Consumed at its declared contract: `record_action(action_ref, actor_ref, credential, data) → event_id | rejected(invalid-credential | invalid-request | recording-failure(step))` for the intent, outcome, refusal, recovery-intended, abandoned, and escalation records, with the `(step)` payload carried on every transcription; `read_record(event_id) → audit_record | not-known` — called where an `event_id` is in hand: by [Resolve] to tell a purged intent from an absent one, and by Generation acceptance check 1 to place an intent inside the horizon. **Audit Trail projects no failure arm on it, so an outage arm on `read_record` is this composition's fourth instance capability requirement**, declared here exactly as the range read's arms are and for the same reason — a constituent that answers `not-known` for a journal it could not reach makes an outage indistinguishable from absence. This page maps it at its one deciding caller: a `read_record` that cannot reach the journal lands `rejected(recording-failure(resolution))` at [Resolve], the retry-safe arm, and never `not-known`. Without the declaration an outage at that purge check reads as absence, which is the substitution §*Lawful destruction is answered before absence* forbids one section away. (The read-backs at [Open] step 4 and [Close] step 3 do *not* use it, and this page said they did: those steps are recovering from an arm that returned no `event_id`, so they read by `invocation_id` through the filtered range read instead.) And, third, the substrate's declared **open-upper-bound sequence-range read** (its own instance capability requirement over Event Log) as the enumeration behind every rebuild and every sweep, **filtered in this composition's code** — selecting on `action_ref`, `invocation_id`, or any payload field is a lookup the substrate routes to a forthcoming Reverse Index pattern and does not itself offer, so every sentence below that says *enumerate the intents* means *read the range, keep the records whose payload matches*. The substrate's `payload_cap`, `reference_length_cap`, and `attestation_id_width` bound every record this composition writes; its `retention_policy` is the horizon of every sweep; its `reconciliation_operator` convention is the model for the service identity below. Attribution, retention, and sealing of every record here are Audit Trail's Invariants 1, 2, and 3; the destruction of a record's payload at the horizon is its Invariant 4 and Invariant 8.

- **The bound act** — not a constituent, and listed here so that no reader looks for one. The act is the adopter's constituent commit call, supplied as a binding (*Bindings*, below), together with the read that tells whether it committed. This composition never names the adopter's constituent and never reads its store except through that bound read. The constituent's own contract governs the commit: its rejection arms, its atomicity over its own write, its idempotence or lack of it.

- **The act's section** — a **critical section keyed by the act**, supplied by the deployment as an instance capability requirement (*Configuration*, `act_section`), with the lease semantics stated there. No constituent grants it: the substrate serializes nothing across calls, and the adopter's constituent serializes at most its own write.

**Instance capability requirements.** Four declared, two optional. The substrate's open-upper-bound sequence-range read — `EventLog.read` through Audit Trail's list-shaped pass-through, over the range `[sequence_high_water + 1 .. open)`, filtered in this composition's code — inherited from Audit Trail's own requirement over its Event Log instance and consumed for every rebuild and every sweep. **Its rejection arms are part of the requirement and are declared here**, because Audit Trail states the pass-through in prose and projects no contract for it, routing only `EventLog.read`'s `invalid-query` — a deployment fault — to itself: the read answers a page of records **with a cursor and a declared page size, and a page-complete signal**, without which no reader on this page can tell a short page from the end of a range and every rebuild is unsound; `invalid-query` for a range or filter this composition built wrong (a fault, surfaced on `compliance_surface`, never retried as-is), and `unavailable` for a journal it could not reach. **And, fourth, an outage arm on `read_record`** (above), which Audit Trail also projects without one. `unavailable` is the arm every read path on this page transcribes — [Open] step 3's `journal-unavailable`, [Reconcile]'s and [Read Invocation]'s of the same name, and the position arms at [Close], [Refuse] and [Resolve] — and without it declared, those **six** transcribe a constituent arm that no page states. **Read-your-writes on that read for every under-section pre-check and read-back**, stated against the event the protocol actually waits on: a record whose `record_action` has **returned** — on any arm that leaves it appended — is visible to every subsequent range read of the instance **from any node**; a record whose reply was lost is visible once `journal_write_bound` has elapsed since the append was issued. The first clause is the load-bearing one and this page previously lacked it: the section is released on the holder's **return**, which is typically well inside `journal_write_bound`, so a waiter admitted at that instant re-reads before a time-bounded guarantee would say anything, sees no closing, and writes the second one (the formal model's `-buggy-visible` twin is this page with only the time-based clause, and the checker rejects it). The substrate's own step 5 tolerates a lagging replica because its fallback is a rebuild that is merely expensive; this composition cannot, because a miss here is followed by a retry that appends the duplicate Invariant 2 forbids. `act_section` (Configuration).

**A deployment that supplies those three has a conforming reconciliation; one that does not has none and this composition does not start.** Two further capabilities are **optional**, each strengthening one named guarantee and each degrading it in a named way when it is absent — `commit_fence` per act kind (*Bindings*), and `journal_fence` on the substrate. Neither is available in this library today, so the degraded readings below are the ones a real deployment gets, and they are written as guarantees rather than as apologies.

**`journal_fence`** — a fence on the composition's *own* writes, standing to the journal exactly as `commit_fence` stands to the adopter's store, and specified to the same three things: a conveyance, a clock, and an edge.

*Conveyance*, declared with it, in either of the two forms `commit_fence` admits: the substrate's `record_action` carries a deadline parameter this composition passes the instant into, or the host applies a request-scoped deadline to every `record_action` this instance issues, which the substrate's contract need not mention. Audit Trail's `record_action(action_ref, actor_ref, credential, data)` carries no such parameter, so the first form is unavailable in this library and a deployment offering the fence offers the second. **The refusal lands on the position rule's existing arm and introduces no new code:** `recording-failure(intent)` at [Open], `recording-failure(outcome)` at [Close]; at a **sweep run's** closing there is no code to land — [Reconcile] exports none for a single act — and the refusal is absorbed into `skipped` with the intent left open for the next run, `recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code)` at [Refuse], `recording-failure(resolution)` at [Resolve]. A fenced-out write is indistinguishable to its caller from any other write that did not land, which is the point: the caller's arm does not change, only the certainty that nothing lands later.

*Clock*: the same two-clock case; the instants are §*Where the allowance goes*, which owns their values and the argument against minting either bare.

*Edge*: **this composition fences two things**, so the effective instant is the **earlier** of the two §*Where the allowance goes* lists — the writer's lease terminus, and the write's own per-write terminus. The second is what makes the read-back after a lost reply sound. Primitive policies conclude absence once the disclosed bound has elapsed since the issue; but `journal_write_bound` is a disclosed bound with headroom, and this page says of relative bounds that they decide nothing, so without the per-write instant a write may land later than the bound and still inside the lease — after the read-back, which then retries and appends the duplicate Invariant 2 forbids. The `-buggy-perwrite` twin is this page without the per-write instant, and the checker rejects it.

**Under `journal_fence = none` — the case in this library today — Invariant 2 degrades to an observable statement, and the page does not pretend otherwise.** What holds is a **records-side property**, stated over what an auditor can read and nothing else: *at quiescence — no invocation in flight and no sweep run mid-pass — at most one closing record names an `invocation_id`, save a declared supersession pair; and where a paused writer's late append lands beside another writer's, both records exist, the act carries `binding_duplicate`, and the composition detects, reports and resolves it rather than preventing it.* An earlier round wrote the first clause as "written by a writer that held the section and **was not paused across its terminus**" and then, in the same sentence, rejected "writes whose issuers were not paused past their lease" as unenumerable — which is the same predicate wearing a different coat. No auditor can enumerate which writers were paused, so the guarantee is stated without them.

Three mechanisms make that checkable, and each is a declared surface of this page rather than a hope about one:

- **[Read Invocation]** carries `binding_duplicate` as a **declared field of its signature**, sets it where two unsuperseded closings — or two intents — name one `invocation_id`, decides the entry's `state` by the precedence rule (supersession first, lowest `sequence_number` among the rest), and returns every duplicate in `records` rather than hiding it.
- **[Reconcile] step 5's duplicate scan** counts closing records per `invocation_id` across the run's own delta and surfaces every act with more than one on `compliance_surface`. It cannot live in step 2: step 1 keeps only *open* intents, and an act with two closings is named by both and is therefore not open — so a scan riding the examine pass would never see the state it exists to find. It rides the delta instead, which is where a late append necessarily appears, the substrate stamping `recorded_at` when the record lands and not when it was issued.
- **Generation acceptance check 2** quantifies over **closing records of all four kinds**, so a second closing of any kind — an escalation beside an outcome, an abandonment beside a refusal — reads as this conformance failure. Quantified over outcomes alone, as an earlier round wrote it, the commonest duplicate on a fenceless kind is invisible to it.

**The intent record is inside this, and that is the part an earlier round of this page missed.** The degraded statement above is written over *closing* records, and the same reasoning applies unchanged to the `<kind>.intended` record: [Open] takes the section, re-reads, writes its intent, and releases on the write's **return** — so a write that lands after the section has passed to the next waiter leaves that waiter's own pre-check reading a journal in which no intent is open, and a **second live intent for one `act_key`** appears. Two acts of one key in flight together carry `pairing_datum` values `probe` cannot separate, and both escalate (Invariant 3).

**The fence is what prevents it, and the lease gate is not.** The formal model settles this against the reading a fresh reader is likely to reach: gating the intent write on `remaining ≥ journal_write_bound` and leaving the fence off still admits the second intent, while leaving the gate off *with* the fence declared does not. A fence refuses a write that would become visible past the writer's terminus, so the intent is visible before the section can pass on, whatever the gate says. The gate is required anyway — it is this page's rule for every other write and it costs nothing — but a deployment that adds the gate and reads it as the remedy has fixed nothing.

**So under `journal_fence = none` a second live intent is reachable, and the three surfaces above do not see it**, because every one of them keys on `invocation_id` and two invocations carry two of those. The surfaces are therefore stated over **both** keys: a duplicate is two unsuperseded closings on one `invocation_id`, *or* two open intents on one `(kind, act_key)`. [Read Invocation] reports the second on its **act-level** `binding_duplicate` field, beside `invocations`, rather than on an entry; [Reconcile] step 5's scan counts open intents per `(kind, act_key)` across its delta; Generation acceptance check 2 fails a second open intent on a key inside the horizon. Without the second key the commonest duplicate a fenceless deployment produces is invisible to all three. **The second key holds only where the act kind declares a `service_identity`** — §*Which closing stands* carries that branch, and it is what keeps a report-only deployment's declared degradation, which [Open] step 3 instructs, from reading as the fenceless duplicate this paragraph is about.

A deployment with the fence has the stronger guarantee and none of this machinery fires.

## Composition logic

### Bindings

This composition is adopted, not called from nowhere: an adopter binds the parameters below once per **act kind**, and the instance's **`bindings`** Configuration entry is the table of bound kinds — configuration, not state — that [Reconcile] reads to find each intent's `completion_bound`, `commit_fence`, `probe`, `service_identity`, `retention_period` (step 1's upper edge) and `<kind>.*` vocabulary (the membership test by which the rebuild classifies a record as an outcome) by its `<kind>`; **unbinding a kind that still has an open intent of it is a refused configuration change** — a deployment-plane operation of the instance and not one of this composition's seven, so it carries no signature block here and exports no code; what it owes is the refusal itself and the reason, on `compliance_surface` — the instance reads `open_invocations` for that kind and rejects the change while any entry stands, because an unbound kind has no `probe` to run, no `completion_bound` to age against and no `retention_period` to bound the pass, so nothing downstream could close it or even enumerate it correctly. Where a kind is nonetheless absent from the table with intents open — a misconfiguration, not a supported transition — [Reconcile] step 1 surfaces them, under the bound stated there: every `<kind>.intended` record whose `<kind>` the table does not serve **and which no closing record of any kind names**, on `compliance_surface`, once per run, using the **instance's** longest `retention_period` as the pass's upper edge since the kind's own is unknown, and closes none of them; Generation acceptance check 3 exempts them — one binding set for *transfer custody*, another for *disclose subset* — and every action of the adopter that commits that kind of act runs through [Open], the bound commit, and [Close]. A binding is a declaration in the adopter's own *Composes* entry for this composition; it replaces the protocol prose the adopter used to carry. The bindings are the whole of what varies between adopters. Anything an adopter needs that is not a binding is a **deviation**, declared as such (*Edge cases*, *Declared deviations*), never an expansion of this contract.

- **`act_key`** — the identity of one act: the field, or tuple of fields, under which two invocations are the same act and must be serialized. A chain, a record, a token, a principal, a `(fanout, principal)` pair. The section (`act_section`) is keyed by it; the sweep pairs by it; the pre-check under the section reads by it. It must be known before [Open] — an act whose key is minted by the commit itself (a genesis, an allocation) binds the key the adopter can name before the commit (the artifact, the requester and resource) and declares the *minted-key* deviation.
- **`commit`** — the adopter's constituent call that makes the act, with its rejection arms partitioned by the adopter into **pre-commit** (the constituent persisted nothing — retry-safe, closed through [Refuse] and returned to the caller under the constituent's own code) and **committed** (the constituent's record exists). The constituent's contract decides the partition; the adopter transcribes it, never paraphrases it into something stronger. **A third partition is the adopter's, not the constituent's: `unknown`** — no reply (a timeout, a dropped connection), or a reply the constituent's contract does not declare as pre-commit. On `unknown` the adopter calls **neither** [Close] nor [Refuse]: it leaves the intent open, calls [Yield] (which releases the section; the pre-check's age rule keeps the act refused until the sweep closes it), and returns `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` to its caller — the position that says *the act may exist; do not re-run* — and the sweep's `probe` decides. [Refuse] may be called only on an arm the constituent declares pre-commit; mapping a lost reply to a refusal closes the intent over an act that may have committed, and no sweep or check would ever look at it again. The binding also declares **`commit_fence`**, which is `none` unless the deployment can supply it as an **instance capability requirement on the constituent's store**: a store-level fence under which a write issued under this act's section is never applied after the section's expiry. Its conveyance is declared with it — either the constituent's own contract carries a deadline parameter the adopter passes the expiry into, or the store applies the fence by policy to every write the adopter's instance issues (a request-scoped deadline the host attaches, which the constituent's contract need not mention) — and its instant and its edge are §*Where the allowance goes*, which owns both and which this binding does not restate. Under a declared fence a `not-committed` answer past that edge is final and the sweep may write `abandoned`. Under `commit_fence = none` — the common case; no atom in this library declares a deadline parameter today, and the walkthrough binds `none` — the sweep never writes `abandoned` for that act kind, only `escalated`, and the abandonment is an operator's through [Resolve] after their own investigation. A relative request timeout is not a fence — the atom's terminus is an instant, and a relative bound constrains only a write issued inside the lease while nothing bounds when a paused process issues one.
- **`pairing_datum`** — the field the intent record and the constituent's record **both carry by construction**, so that `probe` can find the act's own record and not an earlier identical act's: the invocation's seam-injected `now` passed *into* `commit` where the constituent accepts a caller-supplied stamp (`disclosed_at = now`), or an adopter-minted nonce passed into `commit` and stored by the constituent, or the constituent-minted id where the adopter's action can carry it back into the outcome and the store exposes it by the intent's parameters. `intent_data` carries the datum; `probe` matches on it; the journal's joins never do (they pair on `invocation_id`, Invariant 3). A seam-injected `now` is a sufficient datum only where no two serialized invocations of one `act_key` can carry the same reading **on any node of the instance** — a declared obligation, and one that clock resolution alone does not meet: two nodes' seams may read the same instant within `clock_skew_allowance`, and the section serializes the invocations without separating their stamps. The failure is safe (`probe` answers `undecidable` and the act is escalated with candidates, never chosen wrongly) but it is a failure, and a nonce avoids it — and a nonce is preferred wherever the store admits one. Where no such datum exists — the store carries nothing the intent can name in advance — the adopter declares `pairing_datum = none`, and `probe` may answer only `undecidable(candidates)` or `not-committed`, never `committed`.
- **`repeatable`** — `yes` or `no`: whether two invocations against one `act_key` are two acts (a transfer, a review) or one act attempted twice (a genesis, a close). [Open]'s `act-landed` refusal fires only for `no`.
- **`probe`** — a read of the adopter's constituent store, keyed by `act_key` and the intent's `pairing_datum`, that answers one question for the sweep: **did this act commit, and what is its outcome?** Its answers are `committed(outcome_action_ref, outcome_data)` — exactly one store record matches the pairing datum, and `outcome_action_ref` names which of the act kind's outcomes it is — `not-committed`, `undecidable(candidates)` — more than one record matches, or the datum is `none` and records match the payload — or **`unavailable`** — the store could not be read (an outage, a timeout, a rejection of the read itself). Every constituent read rejection maps to `unavailable`, **never** to `not-committed`. `outcome_data` is what the store can **re-derive** — the minted id, the resulting state, the stamps the constituent wrote — and nothing the store does not hold; where the outcome's authoritative datum exists nowhere but in the dead invocation's memory, `probe` answers `undecidable` and the sweep does not invent it.
- **`completion_bound`** — the longest a conforming invocation of this act kind takes between [Open]'s section take and [Close]'s last write, including the constituent round-trip. It is the lease length of the section, the lower edge of the sweep, and the first term of the liveness inequality. Read against the seam clock [Open] was injected with.
- **`journal`** — the Audit Trail instance the records go to — one per Recoverable Invocation instance, shared by every act kind it serves, so that `sequence_high_water` is one mark — and the `action_ref` vocabulary of this act kind: `<kind>.intended`, the **enumerated list** of its outcome refs `<kind>.<outcome>` (the rebuild classifies a record as an outcome by membership in this list), `<kind>.refused`, with the composition's own `<kind>.recovery_intended`, `<kind>.abandoned`, and `<kind>.escalated` derived from it; `<kind>` is never `audit`, which the substrate reserves. **The whole `<kind>.*` vocabulary is placed under one fixed retention policy** — `retention_period`, a per-kind binding: a deployment obligation on the substrate's `retention_policy` that it resolves to one fixed `policy_ref` of that period for every `<kind>.*` record whatever its `actor_ref` or payload — **a selector on the `<kind>` prefix of `action_ref` and nothing else**, since `retention_period` is a per-kind binding while the `journal` binding puts every kind of an instance on one Audit Trail instance; where a substrate's `retention_policy` admits only one `policy_ref` per instance and no selector, an instance can serve only kinds that share a period, and **kinds with different periods need different Recoverable Invocation instances** — not merely different journals, because `sequence_high_water` is one scalar per instance (*Composition state*) and two journals under one instance would need two marks. Listed under the externally-clearable checks. The sweep's upper edge is derived from it, never read from a record's own placement: an intent retained late (its [Open] proceeded past `step-4`, and the substrate's own reconciliation placed it days later) would carry a `retention_until` *after* its closing records', and an edge read from it would examine an intent whose closings are already purged.
- **`service_identity`** — a registered actor in the substrate's Actor Identity registry (`actor_ref` and `credential`) under which the sweep writes, or **`none`**. With `none` the sweep is **report-only**: it writes nothing, every open intent past the bound is surfaced through the adopter's declared compliance surface, and the closure invariant's liveness arm degrades from *closed* to *surfaced* (Invariant 4). The adopter says which.
- **`outcome_envelope`** — the largest outcome payload this act kind can write, sized by the adopter from its own caps (every set-valued field capped; every reference under `reference_length_cap`), so that [Open] can size the outcome and the compensation before the intent lands (Invariant 6).
- **`retry_terminus`** — what bounds an invocation's **retry** of its own write. `lease` (the default: the retry runs while the section's lease has `journal_write_bound` left) or the declared deviation `counted(n)`. Renamed from `terminus`, which promised more than it delivers: **the terminus is the lease under both values** — the section's expiry is what ends an invocation's standing to write, and this binding never moves it for an adopter that consumes no clock and bounds its in-invocation **retry** by attempts. Under either, [Close] confirms through `remaining` that the section is still held with `journal_write_bound` of lease left before every write — a host query, not a clock read — so `counted(n)` changes the retry bound at [Close] step 3 and nothing else.

### Composition state

**Five elements, each carrying its Contract classification** per [`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Composition state — *derived index*, with the named rebuild procedure that regenerates it from constituent stores, or *extraction-pending*, with the named proposed atom that will own the truth. **Four are wholly derived indexes** — `open_invocations`, `act_closings`, `sweep_closed_ids` and `sequence_high_water` — and all four are rebuilt from one sequence-range read over the journal, filtered to the bound kinds' records, whose `kind` and `act_key` the composition writes itself (*Primitive policies*). **One is wholly extraction-pending** — `findings`, the register of conditions this instance surfaced, which is truth no constituent store carries. `act_section` is listed last and is not composition state at all.

- **`open_invocations`** — map from **`(kind, act_key)`** — the pair `act_section` is keyed by, and for the same reason: two kinds with byte-equal keys are two acts, so a map keyed on `act_key` alone would answer a `custody` invocation with a `ledger` intent and refuse it `act-in-flight` naming a foreign `invocation_id`, and [Reconcile] step 1, which rebuilds this map across every bound kind in one pass, would find two kinds' intents under one entry and not know which section to take — to the **set** of intent records (`intent_event_id`, `invocation_id`, the payload) of every invocation of that act that has opened and not yet closed, refused, been abandoned, or been escalated — a singleton under a conforming section, and several only under `service_identity = none`, where [Open] proceeds past an old open intent; [Close] and [Refuse] read the set by their own `invocation_id`. **`not-open` is decided from neither this map nor any journal read, and not by this composition at all** — it is a precondition on the caller ([Not Open], which owns the rule and names what happens when a caller does not enforce it), and this entry said otherwise for two rounds: a map rebuilt from a range read is exactly the journal read the code's own rule forbids, and on a node whose rebuild has not caught another node's intent it would answer `not-open` for a live invocation. **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild:* the substrate's sequence-range read filtered to this act kind's `<kind>.intended` records, less those a later record names through `invocation_id` (`<kind>.<outcome>`, `<kind>.refused`, `<kind>.abandoned`, `<kind>.escalated`). Populated by [Open], cleared by [Close], [Refuse], and the sweep. Read by [Open]'s pre-check, [Close]'s pre-check, and the sweep. **Past the horizon** it is *not needed*: an intent whose payload the substrate has destroyed is outside every sweep and every check (Invariant 7), and the map is bounded to intents younger than the journal's retention horizon — the fourth answer of §*A derived index splits at the horizon* is not needed here because no claim reads the map past the horizon.
- **`act_closings`** — map from `(kind, act_key)` to the act's **closing records**, whoever wrote them: the invocation's outcome, the sweep's, a refusal, an abandonment, an escalation, an operator's resolution. **Contract classification: derived index**, rebuilt from the same sequence-range read. It was missing, which left three surfaces reading records nothing retained: [Open] step 3's `act-landed` guard for a `repeatable = no` kind, [Resolve]'s supersession read, and [Read Invocation]. Every read on this page is a **delta** read above `sequence_high_water`, so a record older than the mark is reachable only from a local map — and the other three maps all discard exactly these records: `open_invocations` keeps intents *less* those a closing names, `sweep_closed_ids` keeps only the ids the **sweep** closed, and `sequence_high_water` is a scalar. Without this map, a genesis outcome at sequence 900 is invisible to an [Open] whose mark stands at 40,000, and the `act-landed(outcome_event_id)` refusal — an exported code with a payload — has no source for its payload and never fires. Past the horizon it is needed for nothing: an act whose records aged out is outside every guard that reads this map. Populated by [Close], [Refuse], [Resolve] and the sweep; read by [Open] step 3, [Resolve]'s supersession check, and [Read Invocation].
- **`sweep_closed_ids`** — the set of `invocation_id`s the **sweep** has closed — an outcome under `recovery = true`, an `<kind>.abandoned`, or an `<kind>.escalated` record naming the id. Named for its writer rather than for its contents, because `act_closings` is the map of *every* writer's closing records and the two were near-homographs for two rounds while different guards read each. **Contract classification: derived index**, rebuilt from the same enumeration; it is the sweep's closed-state marker (the shape the substrate's own `compensated_attestations` carries), consulted under the section so that two sweep runs — restart and cadence, one node or two — never both close one act: `open_invocations` says an intent is open, `sweep_closed_ids` says which closing the sweep already wrote for it, and the second re-read is what a run that lost its lease mid-closure relies on next run. Past the horizon it is truth-bearing for nothing: an act whose closing record aged out is outside the sweep, and a duplicate closing record for it is unreachable because the sweep does not examine it. An intent whose payload the substrate has destroyed is unreadable by the filter that builds `open_invocations`, so it is in neither map and no report of it is owed (Invariant 7).
- **`sequence_high_water`** — the highest `sequence_number` the composition has read from the journal. Every re-read on this page reads the delta above it and **advances the local maps by that delta** — never replaces them — which is correct only if the range read is **prefix-closed**: no record with a `sequence_number` at or below the mark becomes visible after the mark has passed it. That is a property of the substrate's read, not of this page, and it is **not** granted by the Event Log invariant this page's rebuild otherwise leans on; Audit Trail's own step 5 admits a lagging replica precisely because its fallback is a full rebuild, which this page's delta rule does not perform. Contiguity cannot be the test — the substrate's `next_sequence_number` counts allocations rather than successful appends, so a gap is normal and a mark that waited for one would pin below the first failed append and never move, degrading every "delta" read to a full enumeration. **The mark advances by age instead:** past a `sequence_number` whose `recorded_at` is older than `journal_write_bound + clock_skew_allowance`, by which time any append that was going to land has landed and become visible. And **each instance performs a full rebuild at process restart and at `reconciliation_cadence` intervals** — the sweep's mandatory restart run already does one — so that a record which arrived below the mark is picked up within a cadence rather than never; and every read of `open_invocations` drops an intent whose `recorded_at + retention_period` has passed (a purge writes no log record, so the horizon is applied on read, not on delta) — so an intent below the mark is already in the map and one above it arrives with the delta; a lost mark rebuilds both maps from the log's beginning, as the substrate's own reconciliation does. **Contract classification: derived index**; past the horizon it is not needed.
- **`findings`** — the register of what the sweep could not close and what the instance could not do, written to the deployment's `compliance_surface` (*Configuration*, which binds the surface and restates none of this contract). **Contract classification: extraction-pending.** No constituent witnessed a surfacing — it leaves no journal record, which is exactly why Generation acceptance check 3's two clearances read this register and not the journal — so no rebuild procedure exists and none can be written. **The named proposed atom is a Condition Register** *(forthcoming)*: a durable register of named conditions, each opening at a first sighting, advancing at every later one, and ceasing when a pass no longer sees it. Until that extraction lands the element is flagged debt, named rather than normalized, and the contract below is the declaration. An earlier round of this page declared four elements and closed the section *there is no composition-owned middle*, which stopped being true the moment this surface acquired a contract, and which would have led an implementer to hold the register in process memory and lose an outage's span across the restart that outage caused.

  **Two obligations attach, and they pull in different directions.** *Durable:* a lost finding is **not** a rebuild trigger the way a lost index entry is — there is nothing to rebuild it from — so the deployment owns persisting it for at least the `retention_period` of the kind a finding names, or the instance's longest for a finding that names none, so that an auditor clearing a check reads a history as long as the journal's stamps. *Outside the atomicity surface, in one direction only:* a surfacing that fails MUST NOT reject a [Reconcile] run that has already landed closings — the run counts the act in `skipped` and the next run surfaces it — and this is the one respect in which the register behaves like an index.

  **A [Finding] is EXACTLY ONE OF two shapes.**

  ```
  Shape:    act finding
  Fields:   {finding, kind, act_key, invocation_id, first_seen, run_id}
  Names:    one act the run could not close
  Dedup on: (invocation_id, finding)
  ```

  ```
  Shape:    instance finding
  Fields:   {finding, kind, first_seen, last_seen, run_id}
  Names:    one condition of the instance, or of one bound kind
  Dedup on: (finding, kind)
  ```

  **`kind` is present on both shapes, and on the instance shape it may be absent.** A condition of one act kind carries it — a store is the adopter's and the adopter is per kind, so one kind's store outage says nothing about another's. A condition of the instance itself does not — the journal is the instance's, and a journal outage stops every kind at once. An earlier round of this page keyed `store-unavailable` on `finding` alone, under which one adopter's store outage blanket-exempted every other kind's intents from bounded closure.

  **Five conditions are named, and no surface may read one this list does not carry.**

  - **`journal-unavailable`** — instance finding, **no `kind`**: the journal could not be read or written this run ([Reconcile] step 1).
  - **`store-unavailable`** — instance finding, **`kind` present**: that kind's adopter's store answered `unavailable` to `probe` ([Reconcile] step 3).
  - **`closure-at-risk`** — act finding: the act's remaining window is below what one more cadence and one more run need ([Reconcile] step 5). Generation acceptance check 3's `service_identity = none` branch reads it by this name.
  - **`binding_duplicate`** — act finding: two closings or two open intents on one act (§*Which closing stands*). A report under `journal_fence = none`; no check reads it.
  - **`unbound-kind`** — act finding: an open intent of a kind this instance no longer binds. It is an *act* finding because every record this composition appends carries `kind` and `act_key` as composition-written fields, so an unbound kind's intent has a key like any other; an earlier round of this page said it had none and made it an instance finding for that reason.

  **A span opens, advances, and closes; a recurrence is a new span.** `first_seen` is set when a condition is first surfaced and never moves. `last_seen` advances on every run that still sees it. A condition **ceases** on the first run that does not see it, and its record **closes** with `last_seen` at the previous run — and a later recurrence opens a **new** record with a fresh `first_seen`, never reopening the closed one. That is load-bearing rather than tidy: check 3's exemption is an overlap test against a span, so one record spanning two outages would exempt every intent in the healthy gap between them. Act findings carry no `last_seen` and no span; the run that closes the act stops writing them.

  **A finding is written once per condition, not once per run.** [Reconcile] step 5 surfaces what it sees each run; the register deduplicates, so a standing condition appears once with its span growing, and "once per run" describes the *write* while the dedup describes the *record*. An earlier round stated the two as if they conflicted. **Order:** per `act_key` only; no total order is needed or claimed.

- **`act_section`** — not composition state, listed so the reader does not look for it here: it is a **[Lease](../atoms/lease.md)**, bound in *Configuration*. Audit Trail describes a section of its own in prose and admits a host that frees it on a holder's death; **this composition requires the atom's semantics, under which no host does that** (Lease Invariant 3, whose argument is the formal model's rejected `-buggy-death` twin). An adopter reading both pages takes the atom's for acts under this composition.

### Configuration

- **`bindings`** — the table of act kinds this instance serves, each with its full binding set (*Bindings*); a deployment unbinds a kind only after resolving every open intent of it. *Default:* none.
- **`act_section`** — an **instance capability requirement**: a **[Lease](../atoms/lease.md) host** the deployment supplies, **shared across every node of the instance** (a section held on one node and not another is no section). The atom carries the semantics — one holder, the terminus is the instant, death is not observable, a waiter's bound is fixed at arrival, standing is answered rather than assumed, and a fence instant carries its allowance. This entry binds it.

  **Key: `(kind, act_key)`.** Two kinds with byte-equal keys are two sections, so a signature that omits the kind names the wrong one — which binds this composition's own signatures, and for two rounds did not: every action but [Reconcile] takes `kind` as its first argument, and `open_invocations` is keyed on the same pair, because an action given only `act_key` has nothing to derive `<kind>.intended` from, no `completion_bound` to read, and no way to tell one kind's open intent from another's under a shared key. [Reconcile] takes no key at all: it enumerates every bound kind in one pass.

  **Holder:** the `invocation_id` for an invocation, the run's own seam-injected id for a sweep run, `operator_run_id` for [Resolve].

  **Duration:** `completion_bound` for an invocation — no shorter, so a conforming invocation is never evicted mid-section; no longer, so a stalled-but-alive holder blocks the sweep for at most the bound — and `closure_latency + journal_write_bound` for a sweep run or an operator.

  **Arms:** the atom's `unavailable` is this page's `rejected(section-unavailable)` ([Section Unavailable]) at [Open] step 2 and at [Resolve]; its `not-held` is discarded by every caller here, which is what [Yield] after an expired lease and [Close] step 1's yield path both rely on. This page binds three of the atom's four operations and **does not use `try_take`**: every take it makes is one it must make.

  **What this composition does with the grant.** It is **taken by [Open] before the intent record and held through [Close]'s last write** — the intent, the bound commit, every in-invocation retry of the outcome, the outcome, the derived-index update. **Its expiry is the invocation's terminus for writes**: an invocation whose lease has expired makes no further write, does not re-take, and discards any constituent reply that arrives after the expiry (the sweep's `probe` will read the store for it); it returns `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` to its caller. **The sweep takes the same section on every `act_key` it examines**, holds it across its pre-check and its closing write, is a holder under the same terminus — a closing write in flight at expiry is left for the next run, which re-reads `sweep_closed_ids` first — and is **reentrant for its own holder alone**: the sweep's `probe` runs as the holder, one hold, one lease.

  **The lease is what ends the hold; it is not what keeps a dead holder's write from landing.** That is the `journal_fence`, and the distinction is this page's central wiring decision rather than the atom's: with the fence declared, Invariant 2 holds whether or not the `remaining ≥ journal_write_bound` gate is there; without it, Invariant 2 fails whether or not the gate is there. The gate is check-then-act — a holder passes it, is paused by a stop-the-world collection or a migration, and issues its write afterwards — so its real work is to stop a holder starting a write the fence will refuse, and to make the yield deterministic rather than discovered at the refusal. The constituent commit is bounded by its own fence (`commit_fence`, *Bindings*). *Default:* none — the composition does not start without it.
- **`journal_write_bound`** — the deployment's disclosed bound on a `record_action` from issue to landing: the substrate's declared `record_action_completion_bound` plus the issue-to-first-commit latency, with headroom. Every journal write under a section is gated on `remaining ≥ journal_write_bound`, the invocation's first included.

  **The act kind's lease must cover everything spent under it** — condition 3 of instance start, which owns the inequality and the argument for it (§*Instance start*). **The sweep's lease is `closure_latency + journal_write_bound`**, stated directly rather than checked, for the reason that section gives. *Default:* none.
- **`reconciliation_cadence`** — the interval at which the sweep runs after its mandatory run at process restart. *Default:* none.
- **`read_bound`** — the deployment's disclosed bound on one **under-section journal read**: the range read [Open] step 3, [Close] step 2, [Refuse] and [Resolve] each make before deciding. It is the term an earlier round of this page left undisclosed while three actions spent it inside a lease, and it enters conditions 3 and 5 of instance start, since a lease must cover the read *and* the write that follows it. *Default:* none.
- **`closure_latency`** — the deployment's disclosed bound on one **whole closure**: the section's re-read, the `probe` round-trip, the `<kind>.recovery_intended` record, the outcome or abandoned record, and the derived-index update — not one write. Its parts are checked against it: condition 5 of instance start (§*Instance start*). *Default:* none.
- **`run_bound`** — the deployment's disclosed bound from a [Reconcile] run's start to the last closing it lands, over the largest backlog the deployment sizes for (a run closes acts concurrently or not as it declares; sequentially, `run_bound` is the backlog times `closure_latency` plus the waits), and a run that outlasts a cadence is one run — the next does not start beside it. It is the term the window's inequality carries **three times** — for the run in flight when the act crossed the edge, for the run that dies, and for the run that closes (§`compensation_window`) — since a closure the inequality bounds is only the run's first. The sweep's own lease is `closure_latency + journal_write_bound`, so a slow closure is never cut off by a fast act's bound — and, unlike the `max` an earlier round wrote, that is a lease a deployment with a slow closure can actually configure; and because the waits term above carries one such lease, the disclosure has a floor: condition 4 of instance start (§*Instance start*). Below it the disclosure is self-contradictory — a run that waits out one holder and then closes one act has already exceeded its own bound — and condition 1, which relies on the waits being inside this term, stops holding. *Default:* none.
- **`compensation_window`** — the duration within which an act whose invocation died must be closed by the sweep (an outcome, an abandoned record, or an escalation) — measured **from the intent record's `recorded_at`**, the act's creation, not from the sweep's detection. **What makes it meetable is conditions 1 and 2 of instance start** (§*Instance start*), which own both inequalities, the three-run derivation the first of them carries, and the enumeration that refuted the two forms this page tried before it. Condition 1 is rearranged as the at-risk threshold of [Reconcile] step 5. *Default:* none.
- **`clock_skew_allowance`** — the allowance under which a reading from one clock is compared to a stamp or an instant from another. Seven clocks meet on this page and one allowance covers every pair, set to cover the widest; **§*Where the allowance goes* owns the list, and owns which comparisons take the allowance at the reading and which instants carry it minted in.** This entry declares the knob and adds nothing to that section. For an auditor the same allowance is the cross-seam tolerance of the checks, not a widening of the liveness bound — which is why `probe` pairs on `pairing_datum` by equality and never on a stamp within a window (§*A stamp from another seam never decides a write alone*). *Default:* none.
- **`intent_candidates_cap`** — the most `store_candidates` an `<kind>.escalated` record names where `probe` answers `undecidable(candidates)`, each a constituent store record reference under `reference_length_cap`; the compensation envelope is sized with it (Invariant 6). Past the cap the act is escalated with the count and the range, not the list. *Default:* none.
- **`retention_period`** — **declared as a per-kind binding** (*Bindings*, the `journal` binding, which states the fixed-policy obligation it places on the substrate's `retention_policy` and the selector form that obligation takes); listed here because it is read as an instance knob by the sweep's upper edge (`recorded_at + retention_period`, [Reconcile] step 1) and by condition 2 of instance start. The binding is normative; this entry is a cross-reference and adds nothing. *Default:* none.
- **`read_cap`** — the most invocations [Read Invocation] returns for one act, and the most records it returns per invocation; older ones are reached by `invocation_id`. *Default:* none.
- **`compliance_surface`** — the **deployment's** declared surface (named the same way wherever it appears on this page; it is the deployment's and never the adopter's, since one instance serves many adopters and a finding names the kind) the deployment supplies for this instance's `findings` (*Composition state*, which owns the contract the surface must meet — the two record shapes, the dedup keys, the span, the durability floor and the per-key order — and which this entry does not restate); the substrate's own alerting obligation is the model. **Two Generation acceptance clearances read it**, both branches of check 3: the `service_identity = none` branch, where the check is that every at-risk intent appears here, and the outage exemption, which is keyed on an instance finding's span. Two further uses are reports rather than clearances and no check reads them — the `binding_duplicate` report under `journal_fence = none`, and the unbound-kind surfacing. *Default:* none.

### Where the allowance goes

**`clock_skew_allowance` IS AUTHORITATIVE FOR every comparison on this page between a reading taken at one seam and a stamp or instant minted at another, and this section IS AUTHORITATIVE FOR where that allowance is spent.** Configuration declares the value. *Bindings*, *Composes*, *Primitive policies* and [Reconcile] bind it here and restate none of it. For two rounds the disposition was stated in three places and one of the three asserted, of a second, the opposite of what the second said.

**Seven clocks meet on this page:** the adopter's seam ([Open] step 3), the sweep's seam ([Reconcile]), the operator's seam ([Resolve]), the reader's seam ([Read Invocation]), the substrate's `recorded_at`, the section host's `expires_at`, and — under a declared `commit_fence` — the constituent store's. One allowance covers every pair, set by the deployment to cover the widest.

**Every cross-seam comparison on this page is EXACTLY ONE OF *applied* or *minted*.**

- An **applied** comparison takes the allowance at the reading. An applied comparison MUST NOT decide a write; it may only exclude a record from a pass.
- A **minted** instant carries the allowance subtracted BEFORE it crosses the seam. A minted instant does decide a write. That is what a fence is, and [Lease](../atoms/lease.md) Invariant 6 owns the rule.

**Minted — three instants, each minted independently.**

```
Instant:   `commit_fence`'s deadline
Minted by: the section host, as the act's `expires_at`
Judged by: the adopter's constituent store
Value:     `expires_at − clock_skew_allowance`
Bound at:  *Bindings*, §`commit`
```

```
Instant:   `journal_fence`'s lease terminus
Minted by: the section host, as the writer's `expires_at`
Judged by: the substrate
Value:     `expires_at − clock_skew_allowance`
Bound at:  *Composes*, §`journal_fence`
```

```
Instant:   `journal_fence`'s per-write terminus
Minted by: the writer's own seam, at the write's issue
Judged by: the substrate
Value:     `issue + journal_write_bound − clock_skew_allowance`
Bound at:  *Composes*, §`journal_fence`
```

**A fence's instant MUST NOT be minted bare.** A judge whose clock lags the minter admits, for the whole of the lag, a write the minter would call late — which is the residue the fence exists to remove. The formal model's `-buggy-skew` twin is this page with a bare instant, and the checker rejects it; with the clocks in step the bare instant holds, so it is the skew and not the margin that decides. The `-buggy-perwrite` twin carries the margin on the lease's instant alone, and the checker rejects that too, which is why *independently* is a rule here and not a preference.

**Applied — every other cross-seam comparison on this page, and each of them only excludes.** [Open] step 3's age of an open intent; the retention drop on every read of `open_invocations`; [Resolve]'s too-young guard; the `sequence_high_water` advance; the sweep's edges and window; [Read Invocation]'s rebuild. *Primitive policies* §`now` lists the four that compare a seam reading against a substrate stamp and says why the fourth, which comes nearest to deciding, is still an exclusion.

**A minted instant is paid for, and this page owns the payments because the atom refuses to.** The atom specifies the instant; what it costs a protocol to carry one is the protocol's business.

- **A fenced lease is shorter than it reads.** A fence pulls the usable terminus in by one allowance, so the operations under the lease have `completion_bound − clock_skew_allowance` to finish in and not `completion_bound`. Condition 3 of instance start charges that allowance wherever either fence is declared (§*Instance start*). One allowance and not two: both fences pull the same terminus in.
- **The sweep's abandon edge is widened by a second allowance.** The sweep MUST NOT write `abandoned` before the examine edge plus one further `clock_skew_allowance`, because it judges on its own seam whether the store's clock has passed the fence. This is a different pair from the minting: the minting crosses host to store, the widening crosses sweep to store, and an earlier round of this page offered the two as alternatives.
- **`journal_write_bound` MUST be disclosed with `clock_skew_allowance` of headroom over the substrate's own worst case wherever `journal_fence` is declared**, since the per-write instant is minted that much ahead. Without the headroom a write that takes the full disclosed bound is fenced out, lands on its position's `recording-failure` arm and is retried — safe, and a liveness cost the deployment did not disclose. **The formal model does not confirm this:** it carries the fence, not the disclosure (Ledger, NOT MODELED).

### Instance start

**This section IS AUTHORITATIVE FOR the conditions of instance start.** Every condition below is checked at start for every bound act kind, and a deployment whose knobs fail any of them does not start. Configuration declares the knobs. Invariant 4, Generation acceptance check 4 and the walkthrough cite these conditions by number and state none of them. For two rounds the set was stated in four places at once: the walkthrough's own numbers breached a condition the page declared eight lines above them, and the check that clears instance start named a subset, so nothing caught it.

**Five conditions, four of them strict.**

1. **The window contains the worst closure.** STRICTLY, `completion_bound + 2 × clock_skew_allowance + 2 × reconciliation_cadence + 3 × run_bound < compensation_window` — `completion_bound` the binding of that act kind, the rest this instance's, and the allowance counted twice for every kind, fenced or not, so that one statement serves all.
2. **The horizon outlasts the window.** STRICTLY, `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance + compensation_window < retention_period`, per act kind.
3. **The act's lease covers everything spent under it.** STRICTLY, `completion_bound > 2 × read_bound + 2 × journal_write_bound + the commit's round-trip`; and ONLY IF the act kind declares `commit_fence` or the substrate declares `journal_fence`, `+ clock_skew_allowance` (§*Where the allowance goes*).
4. **A run contains one wait and the closure after it.** NOT STRICTLY, `run_bound ≥ max(completion_bound, closure_latency + journal_write_bound) + closure_latency`.
5. **The closure bound covers the operations it is disclosed over.** STRICTLY, `closure_latency > read_bound + 2 × journal_write_bound + the probe's round-trip`.

**There is no sixth condition on the sweep's lease.** An earlier round carried one — `max(completion_bound, closure_latency)` to exceed `closure_latency + journal_write_bound` — which is unsatisfiable whenever `closure_latency` reaches `completion_bound`, since it reduces to `0 > journal_write_bound`. Every instance that could start therefore had `completion_bound > closure_latency`, the `max` was identically `completion_bound`, and the case the check existed to protect — a closure slower than the act's own bound, which is the ordinary shape of a cross-region probe — could not be configured at all. The lease is stated directly instead, as `closure_latency + journal_write_bound`, and needs no check to be met.

**Why condition 1 carries three `run_bound` terms.** A budgeted death involves three runs and not two. The first is the run *in flight when the act crossed the examine edge*: it reads `now` once at its own seam under the allowance, so a run that started just before `recorded_at + completion_bound + 2 × clock_skew_allowance` may lawfully leave the act out of its pass — and then run that pass to its end, since a run that outlasts a cadence is one run and the next does not start beside it. The second is the run that examines the act and dies, which may die anywhere inside its own pass and is not obliged to have reached this act first. The third is the run that closes it. Two cadences for the same reason, one after each of the first two. *Cadence no longer than the window* is satisfied by a deployment that breaches on every orphan, and is not the rule.

**This page carried one `run_bound` term and one cadence for two rounds, and both were wrong.** It charged a death only the dead run's lease — the cost of a run that dies at the instant it starts — and charged the run in flight at the edge nothing at all. An enumeration of the schedules this page's own timeline admits, over four hundred parameter tuples, breaches the old form on 420 of 432 and breaches a one-term correction on 396; the form above holds on every tuple that also meets condition 4, with one tick of slack and no more. **The formal model could not have caught this and is not at fault:** it carries the leases, the fences and the window, but it has no backlog, so it has no `run_bound` to mis-charge. What it measures — that one budgeted death raises the smallest sufficient window by exactly the sweep's lease, three, five and seven ticks for sweep leases of three, five and seven against a `completion_bound` of three, and that with no death budgeted the sweep's lease does not enter at all — is true of a world with one act in it, and the term above is what a backlog adds to that world.

**Why condition 2 is independent of condition 1.** Without it the sweep's upper edge can fall inside the window: [Reconcile] step 1 drops an intent at `recorded_at + retention_period` and Invariant 7 forbids examining it after, so an orphan leaves the sweep's range before the window it was promised has elapsed, is closed by nobody, and is then removed from checks 1 to 3 by check 7 — the quiet inconsistency Invariant 4's safety arm says cannot happen. The first condition makes closure fast enough; the second keeps the act readable long enough to close.

**Why condition 3 charges four operations and not one.** [Open]'s under-section pre-check read and its intent write, then the commit, then [Close]'s re-read and its outcome write, all inside one lease. An earlier round charged one `journal_write_bound` and the commit alone, which passes on deployments where [Close] yields at its first step on *every* invocation: `completion_bound = 5 s`, `journal_write_bound = 2 s`, a 2 s commit clears `5 > 4` and then runs out of lease with the outcome unwritten.

**Why condition 4 takes a `max`.** `run_bound`'s "plus the waits" is one holder's remaining lease per act (*Configuration*, §`act_section`), so the wait a dead holder imposes on the run after it is already inside `run_bound`, and charging it again in condition 1 would overstate the window. That reasoning holds only if the disclosed `run_bound` is large enough to contain one such wait and the closure that follows it — where it is not, condition 1 is unsound and breaches on exactly the tuples whose `run_bound` falls below a lease plus a closure. **The holder may be either kind, and an earlier round of this page charged only the shorter.** An invocation holds for `completion_bound`; a dead sweep run or an operator holds for `closure_latency + journal_write_bound`; condition 3 forces `completion_bound` above `2 × read_bound + 2 × journal_write_bound + the commit`, so on an ordinary deployment the invocation's lease is the longer of the two and the old form's `2 × closure_latency + journal_write_bound` charged the sweep's. The walkthrough is the case: `max(30, 15) + 12 = 42 s`, against the `2 × 12 + 3 = 27 s` the old form asked for. **This is not hygiene, and the enumeration says so.** Re-run over the same 432 tuples under each floor in turn: the old floor admits 324 of them and condition 1 breaches on **15**; condition 4 admits 288 and condition 1 breaches on **none**. The enumeration had been measuring the `max` form since the round that wrote it while this page's prose asked for the weaker one, so the two disagreed for two rounds and a reader found it in the prose alone. A second run dying inside one window is a conformance failure [Reconcile] step 5 surfaces, not a case the window covers.

**Why condition 5 exists at all.** A closure contains one under-section read and two journal writes whatever else it contains, so a `closure_latency` below their sum is a disclosure contradicting the operations it is disclosed over — and the sweep's lease, which is built on it, inherits the contradiction: the run passes the `remaining ≥ journal_write_bound` gate for its first closing write and fails it for its second, writes nothing further, counts the act in `skipped`, and repeats that on every subsequent run, so the act is never closed by the sweep at all. This page stated the rule for one round and did not check it, and its own walkthrough breached it.

### Primitive policies

- **`act_key`** — opaque, adopter-typed; byte-identity equality as the section's key and the map's key; never normalized here. Capped by the adopter under `reference_length_cap`. **It is a composition-written field of every record this composition appends and is never recovered from an adopter's payload.** An earlier round of this page said its components rode the intent payload, which is the adopter-constructed and composition-opaque `intent_data`, and declared no component-to-key extractor anywhere — so both derived indexes, which are keyed `(kind, act_key)` and rebuilt from these records, had nothing to key on after a restart that lost `sequence_high_water`, and [Open] step 3's pre-check would then read empty for every key and write a second live intent for a key that already had one.
- **`invocation_id`** — minted nowhere in this composition or its adopter: **injected at the adopter's input/output seam alongside `now`** ([`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Logic Confinement Principle), fresh per invocation and **unique across every node of the instance for the journal's lifetime** (the join in Generation acceptance check 2 needs no less), carried in the payload of every record this composition writes for the invocation — intent, outcome, refusal — and by the sweep's records naming it. It is the pairing key of Invariant 3 and the only field any check joins on. Opaque; byte-identity.
- **`actor_ref`, `credential`** — the adopter's caller, passed through to the substrate's `record_action` at the intent record, where Actor Identity validates the credential against the registry **before the bound commit** (Invariant 1). Shape-validated here as non-empty only; never inspected. Capped by the adopter under `reference_length_cap` before [Open], so the intent record's `invalid-request` arm is not reachable from an over-long reference.
- **`intent_data`, `outcome_data`** — adopter-constructed payloads. `intent_data` carries the invocation's parameters and its `pairing_datum` (the seam-injected `now` the adopter will pass into `commit`, or its nonce), and **no field the substrate or the constituent will stamp or mint itself** — no `recorded_at` (the substrate stamps the record at its own seam), no constituent-minted id (none exists yet). `outcome_data` carries what the act produced; the composition itself writes `invocation_id`, `intent_event_id`, `kind`, and `act_key` into every record it appends — intent, outcome, refusal, and the sweep's — so that every rebuild reads them from the composition's own fields. **The four steps that specify a payload each write them explicitly** ([Open] step 4, [Close] step 3, [Refuse] step 3, [Reconcile] step 3), and for two rounds three of them did not, which is what this sentence was asserting against. Both are sized at [Open] against `outcome_envelope` and the substrate's `payload_cap` — measured as the substrate measures it, the serialized envelope `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` with the longer of the caller's and the service identity's `actor_ref`, the substrate's `attestation_id_width`, and the framing — the outcome, not the intent, being the larger, and the sweep's two closings larger still and sized separately, since no record carries both — the recovered outcome (`outcome_data` plus `recovery`, `acting_actor_ref`) and the escalation (`store_candidates` plus the same), the bound being the larger of the two; [Open] refuses `invalid-request` for an act whose largest record would not fit (Invariant 6).
- **`operator_run_id`** — the seam-injected identifier [Resolve] holds the section under, minted per operator call exactly as a sweep run's own id is; it is the holder, never the operator's `actor_ref`, which attests the record instead. **`now` is injected at the same seam and at the same moment**, once per [Resolve] call, and is the reading its too-young guard compares against the substrate's `recorded_at` under `clock_skew_allowance`. The operator's seam is a seam of this composition like the adopter's and the sweep's; [Resolve] reads no clock of its own, exactly as no other action does.
- **`now`** — the seam-injected reading the adopter's invocation carries; no action signature on this page takes it, and [Open] consumes it and `invocation_id` as seam inputs the way every composition in this library does — **`invocation_id` injected once per invocation, at [Open], and carried by the adopter's action into [Close] and [Refuse] as a parameter of each**, which is why it appears in their signatures and `now` does not ([`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Logic Confinement Principle). [Open] uses `now` for exactly one comparison — step 3's age of an open intent against the substrate's `recorded_at`, under `clock_skew_allowance` — and stamps nothing with it: the substrate stamps `recorded_at` at its own seam. **Four comparisons of a seam reading against a substrate stamp exist on this page, and they are not all alike.** Step 3's *excludes* an intent from the in-flight arm. The retention drop on every read of `open_invocations` (*Composition state*) *excludes* an intent past the horizon. [Resolve]'s too-young guard *excludes* an `abandoned` disposition on an intent that is not yet past the edge, on the operator's own seam reading. The `sequence_high_water` advance (*Composition state*) is the fourth and the only one that comes near deciding, since what the mark has passed is what a later pre-check does not re-read — and it is kept on the safe side of Clock semantics' *only excludes, never decides* by the full rebuild every restart and every cadence, which puts back anything a mis-set mark skipped over. **No write on this page is decided by a cross-seam comparison except at a declared fence** — §*Where the allowance goes*, which owns which comparisons are applied and which instants are minted, and which lists these four among the applied. The sweep reads `now` once per run at its own seam, for the edges and the window, under the same allowance. Under the `counted(n)` deviation an adopter still carries `now` for step 3; the deviation removes the clock from [Close]'s retry only.

**The `record_action` rejection-mapping rule, by position.** Every record this composition writes is one `AuditTrail.record_action`, and the substrate's arms map by the call's position relative to the bound commit (§*A transcribed rejection arm keeps its payload*). **At the intent record — nothing has committed:** `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`, a clean pre-state refusal, nothing written anywhere; `invalid-request` → `rejected(invalid-request)`, a deployment fault with four sources the token does not distinguish — the substrate's own input check at its step 1, Actor Identity's through its step 2, its payload fault at step 3, and its retention configuration at step 4 — of which only the retention source leaves the intent **appended** — so [Open] reads back by `invocation_id` before it reports: found → proceed with a hard alert; absent → refuse; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)` → `rejected(recording-failure(intent))`, the one retry-safe arm; `recording-failure(step-4)` → the intent **is** appended and attested, only its retention placement failed — read its `intent_event_id` back and proceed with a hard alert on the unretained record, never retry (a retry appends a second intent). **At the outcome record — the act has committed and no arm can refuse it, only report it:** `recording-failure(step-4)` and the retention-source `invalid-request` → the outcome **is** appended; read its `event_id` back by `invocation_id` and return success with a hard alert; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)` → retry under the section until it lands or the terminus, then `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` — the act exists, the record is owed, the sweep will close it; `invalid-credential` → reachable only as a mid-flight revocation (the same credential validated at the intent moments earlier): the invocation cannot land the outcome under that credential and the sweep lands it under the service identity — with `none`, surfaced. **A `record_action` whose reply is lost — a timeout, a dropped connection — is `unknown` at every position, exactly as the bound `commit` is, and is never retried blind:** the composition waits until `journal_write_bound` has elapsed since the issue (as `remaining` reports it), reads back by `invocation_id` (this paragraph's read-back, under the read-your-writes requirement in *Composes*), adopts the record if it landed, and only then retries. The read-back is the substrate's range read filtered to this `invocation_id` in composition code — the match is exact, because the id is seam-injected and on the payload; the read's completeness rests on Event Log's read consistency (Event Log Invariant 5, through Audit Trail Invariant 5), and the read is read-your-writes by instance requirement (*Composes*), because a lagging replica here would be followed by an append; a miss after a `step-4` or retention-source `invalid-request` arm is therefore an absence, once `journal_write_bound` has elapsed since the issue. Its lower bound is a per-instance high-water mark the composition keeps (a derived index: the highest `sequence_number` it has read; lost → the read starts at the log's beginning, as the substrate's own reconciliation does); the intent's own `sequence_number` and `recorded_at`, which `record_action` does not return, come from **the filtered range read**, which returns both on every event it yields — not from `read_record`, which this page calls only where an `event_id` is in hand and a *payload* is what is wanted ([Resolve]'s purge check, and Generation acceptance check 1). An outcome-position `invalid-request` whose read-back finds nothing — the substrate's payload fault at its step 3, or an outcome that outgrew the envelope — lands `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` with a hard alert: the act exists, its record is owed, and the sweep will write it from `probe`.

### Action wiring

Six actions and one read. [Open], [Close], [Refuse], and [Yield] are called by an adopter's action around its bound commit; [Reconcile] is the sweep, called by the deployment's scheduler and at every restart; [Resolve] is the operator's close; [Read Invocation] is the read. Every action's signature exports the position of every code that could land on more than one side of the commit — [Recording Failure] carries `intent | outcome | refusal | resolution` — (§*A composition's own rejection arm carries the retry bit*). **What an adopter's signature transcribes:**

| Source | Code the adopter exports | What the caller does with it |
|---|---|---|
| [Open] | `invalid-credential` | nothing was written anywhere; the credential is the caller's to fix |
| [Open] | `invalid-request` | the act's inputs or its outcome's size; the caller's to fix |
| [Open] | `act-in-flight(invocation_id)` | another invocation of this act is live or awaiting the sweep; retry later |
| [Open] | `act-landed(outcome_event_id)` | the act is done; take the adopter's own re-entry arm, not a second act |
| [Open] | `section-unavailable` | the section could not be taken inside the holder's lease; retry |
| [Open] | `journal-unavailable` | the pre-check's journal read failed; nothing written; retry |
| [Open] | `recording-failure(intent)` | the intent record did not land; nothing committed; the whole action may be retried |
| the bound `commit`'s pre-commit partition | the constituent's own code, after [Refuse] has landed the refusal | the act was refused and the attempt is recorded |
| [Refuse] | `already-accounted(closing_event_id)` | another writer already closed this invocation; report the act as accounted for, do not re-run |
| [Refuse] | `recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code)` | the refusal record did not land; the sweep records the attempt instead; never re-run the act — the second slot carries the constituent's own pre-commit code, which is what a caller needs to tell a refused act from an unrecorded one |
| the `unknown` partition, after [Yield] | `recording-failure(outcome)` | the act may exist; its record is the sweep's; never re-run |
| [Close] | `recording-failure(outcome)` | the same |

An adopter exports no other code for the protocol's sake. [Resolve]'s codes are the operator's and are not transcribed by an adopter; [Reconcile]'s `journal-unavailable` goes to the deployment's scheduler. **[Read Invocation] is not in the table because it is not called around the commit** — but an adopter's *re-entry* arm does call it, and it answers `journal-unavailable` as well as `not-known`: the two are the act's state being unreadable *for now* and the act having no readable intent at all, and a re-entry arm that collapses the first into the second re-runs an irreversible act whose record is sitting in the journal.

**The shape of an adopter's action, in one paragraph.** Validate the inputs the adopter owns (its caps, its formats) → [Open] (the section, the sizing, the intent record, the pre-check) → the bound `commit` (the adopter's constituent call: its pre-commit arms land through [Refuse], its committed arms through [Close], and `unknown` through neither — the intent stays open for the sweep and the caller receives `recording-failure(outcome)`) → return. Every guard the adopter's constituent fires *after* the intent — `not-current-custodian`, `already-closed`, `archived` — is an authenticated attempt the trail keeps, closed through [Refuse] so that the intent does not stand open. An adopter's action has no other write to the journal for this act.

#### `open`

```
open(kind, act_key, actor_ref, credential, intent_data) →
    {invocation_id, intent_event_id}
  | rejected(
      invalid-credential
    | invalid-request
    | act-in-flight(invocation_id)
    | act-landed(outcome_event_id)
    | section-unavailable
    | journal-unavailable
    | recording-failure(intent)
    )
```

Opens one invocation of the act: takes the section, sizes the records, writes the intent record — which is where the caller's credential is verified — and returns the pair the adopter carries through its commit to [Close].

Steps:

1. Validate `act_key`, `actor_ref`, `credential` non-empty per Primitive policies, and `actor_ref` not the act kind's `service_identity` — the service identity closes acts and never opens them (Generation acceptance check 5 rests on it); any failure → `rejected(invalid-request)`. Size `intent_data` against the substrate's `payload_cap` as Primitive policies measures it (the kind's `outcome_envelope` plus the compensation fields is a per-kind constant, sized once at instance start); over → `rejected(invalid-request)`. Stop. Nothing is written.
2. **Take the act's section** — `take((kind, act_key), invocation_id, completion_bound)`. The take is the lease's start; its length is the act kind's `completion_bound`. The take blocks at most the current holder's remaining lease and then fails; a failed take → `rejected(section-unavailable)` ([Section Unavailable]) — nothing written, retry-safe; the section's own name, not the journal's arm.
3. **Pre-check under the section.** Re-read the journal from `sequence_high_water` for this `act_key` — a journal read under the section, not a local map hit, because on a multi-node instance a local absence is not a miss and would not trigger the rebuild (*Composition state*, the rebuild procedure) — and take `open_invocations[(kind, act_key)]` from it. An open intent younger than `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance` → release, `rejected(act-in-flight(invocation_id))` ([Act In Flight]): another invocation of this act is between its intent and its outcome. The refusal is not what keeps Invariant 2: that invariant is quantified per `invocation_id`, and two concurrent invocations carry distinct ids, so neither could violate it. What a second live intent would break is **pairing**: two acts of one `act_key` in flight together can carry `pairing_datum` values `probe` cannot tell apart, and the answer degrades to `undecidable` for both (Invariant 3, and the `pairing_datum` binding's uniqueness obligation). An open intent older than that belongs to a dead or yielded invocation and is the sweep's: release, `rejected(act-in-flight(invocation_id))` likewise — the caller waits for the sweep, which closes it within `compensation_window`. **Under `service_identity = none` this older-than arm does not fire** (the younger-than arm always does)**:** nothing will close the old intent, so [Open] proceeds past it — the old intent stays open, surfaced on `compliance_surface` until an operator closes it through [Resolve], and the new invocation's records pair on their own `invocation_id` (Invariant 3). **The several open intents this leaves are that deployment's declared degradation and are not a `binding_duplicate`** (§*Which closing stands*, the second key's `service_identity = none` branch, which is what keeps the three duplicate surfaces from reporting this page's own instruction as a conformance failure) — because a report-only deployment that blocked every crashed act's key until the journal purged its payload would have traded one orphan for an unusable key. Then, for an act kind bound `repeatable = no`, read the act's latest outcome **from `act_closings`** (*Composition state*) — not from the delta, which begins above the mark and cannot see a closing older than it: an outcome for this `act_key` → release, `rejected(act-landed(outcome_event_id))` ([Act Landed]) — the act has already been done and the adopter's re-entry (its own `already-*` arm) is the path, not a second act. This guard is journal-bounded: an outcome past the retention horizon is unreadable here, and the constituent's own `already-*` arm at the commit is the authoritative guard; this one is the cheap refusal in front of it. **This read has a disclosed bound like every other operation on this page** — `read_bound` (Configuration) — and it is inside the lease, so an [Open] whose read exhausts what the lease has left writes nothing: release, `rejected(section-unavailable)`, retry-safe. An earlier round left the read unbounded and the intent write ungated, and both are corrected here. **Where the read itself fails** — the journal unreachable, the substrate's range read erroring — the step has no answer and does not guess one: release, `rejected(journal-unavailable)` ([Journal Unavailable]), nothing written, retry-safe. This arm is not decoration. Without it the cheapest conforming implementation reads a failed range read as an empty result and proceeds, which appends a second live intent for one `act_key` — precisely the state this step exists to prevent, and the one that degrades `probe` to `undecidable` for both invocations (Invariant 3). It is a **read** failure and therefore not `recording-failure(intent)`, which names a write that did not land; the two are both retry-safe and pre-commit, and a deployment that cannot tell an outage from a rejected write cannot act on either.
4. **Intent record**, gated on `remaining ≥ journal_write_bound` like every other write on this page (below `journal_write_bound`, release and `rejected(section-unavailable)`; the intent is not the exception an earlier round made it). `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = <kind>.intended, actor_ref, credential, data = intent_data ∪ {invocation_id, kind, act_key})` → `intent_event_id`. Arms per the position rule's intent position: `invalid-credential` → release, `rejected(invalid-credential)`, nothing committed anywhere — **this is the seam at which authentication precedes commitment** (Invariant 1); `invalid-request` → read back by `invocation_id`: found (retention source) → proceed with a hard alert; absent → release, `rejected(invalid-request)`; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)` → release, `rejected(recording-failure(intent))`; `recording-failure(step-4)` → read the id back (Primitive policies, *the read-back*) and proceed with a hard alert.
5. Populate `open_invocations[(kind, act_key)]` with the intent. Return `{invocation_id, intent_event_id}`; the section stays held.

#### `close`

```
close(kind, act_key, invocation_id, intent_event_id, actor_ref, credential,
      outcome_action_ref, outcome_data) →
    {outcome_event_id, landed_by: invocation | sweep | operator}
  | rejected(
      not-open
    | recording-failure(outcome)
    )
```

Writes the act's outcome record after the bound commit has returned, under the section [Open] took, and releases the section. The caller's `actor_ref`, `credential` and `intent_event_id` are the adopter's action's own — the last of them from the pair [Open] returned, which the adopter already carries — and this composition retains none of them between [Open] and [Close]. **`actor_ref` MUST be the value [Open] was given for this invocation.** Generation acceptance check 2 reads an outcome carrying no `recovery` whose `actor_ref` differs from its intent's as a conformance failure of shape, and this composition retains nothing to enforce it with, so like [Not Open] it is the caller's to hold — and unlike [Not Open] it is caught, one retention period later, by an auditor. **Every record either action writes names `intent_event_id`, so both actions take it**: an earlier round wrote it into the payloads and left it out of the signatures, which forces a generator either to omit it (breaking Generation acceptance check 1, which resolves the intent from it) or to derive it from `invocation_id`, and every route from an id to an `event_id` on this page reads the intent's payload — the route [Resolve] takes an argument precisely to avoid. The one action in this composition that can find its own work already done.

Steps:

1. `remaining((kind, act_key), invocation_id)` — confirm the section is still held by this invocation with at least `journal_write_bound` of lease left, a host query. Less than that, or `none`, means this invocation has **yielded** — or was never opened at all, which this step cannot tell apart and which step 0 is the caller's precondition against ([Not Open]): it writes nothing, discards the commit's reply, and returns `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` — the act exists (the commit returned) and its record is the sweep's (`act_section`, the terminus rule). This step is the same under every `retry_terminus` binding — the binding bounds the retry, never the lease.
2. **Pre-check under the section — proceed as landed.** Re-read the journal from `sequence_high_water` for this `invocation_id` (*Composition state*, the rebuild procedure): **any closing record** already naming this `invocation_id` — an outcome, an abandonment, or an escalation — ends this invocation's write. An outcome is **adopted as this invocation's own**: release, return `{outcome_event_id, landed_by = sweep}`, or `landed_by = operator` where it carries `resolved_by`. An abandonment or an escalation is *not* adoptable (it says the act was not accounted for, and this invocation cannot now say otherwise): release and return `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))`, leaving the correction to [Resolve]'s `supersedes` path. Reading only for an outcome here would miss both — and the sweep writes an escalation for every uncertain act on a fenceless kind, which is the common case. **Where this re-read fails**, the invocation writes nothing and returns `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))`, its position's own arm and *not* [Open]'s `journal-unavailable`: the act has committed, so what the caller must not do — re-run it — is already fixed by the position, and the intent stays open for the sweep exactly as it does for every other reason [Close] gives up. The same holds at [Refuse] (`recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code)`) and at [Resolve] (`recording-failure(resolution)`). [Open] alone needs a code of its own, because at [Open] nothing has committed and the caller's next move depends on which of the two failed, the read or the write. **This pre-check fires either way; what a declared `journal_fence` changes is the fate of the write, not the reachability of the read** — the paused invocation the next paragraph describes reaches this step under any configuration. Under conforming section semantics and a declared fence the arm's *write* cannot land: the sweep holds the section only after this invocation's lease expired, step 1 yields before a write could straddle it, and the fence refuses any write this invocation issues afterwards. **Where the act kind's deployment has no `journal_fence`, the arm is reachable and load-bearing**, not defence in depth — a paused invocation passes step 1, wakes after the sweep has closed the act, and this re-read is what stops it appending a second outcome. It is a mitigation and not a repair: the re-read is itself check-then-act, and an invocation paused *between* this step and its write appends beside the sweep's record whatever this step read (Invariant 2's declared degradation, Composes).
3. **Outcome record.** `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = outcome_action_ref, actor_ref, credential, data = outcome_data ∪ {invocation_id, intent_event_id, kind, act_key})` → `outcome_event_id`. Arms per the position rule's outcome position: `step-4` and the retention-source `invalid-request` → appended; read back by `invocation_id`, proceed with a hard alert; any other `invalid-request` → `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` with a hard alert (the act exists; the sweep will escalate it as `outcome-unrecordable`, since it cannot shrink what it re-derives); `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)`, or a lost reply → **re-run step 2's pre-check first** (a landed record is adopted, never doubled), then retry while `remaining` is at least `journal_write_bound` (under `counted(n)`, at most `n` attempts); below that or at the last attempt → release, `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))`, the sweep's; `invalid-credential` (mid-flight revocation) → release, `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` — the sweep lands it under the service identity, or surfaces it under `none`.
4. Remove this `invocation_id` from `open_invocations[(kind, act_key)]`; release the section. Return `{outcome_event_id, landed_by = invocation}`.

**`not-open`** ([Not Open]) is step 0 of [Close] and [Refuse], decided from the invocation's own [Open] result — the `intent_event_id` [Open] returned — and never from a journal read: an `invocation_id` this invocation did not open is an adopter's programming error, not a protocol state, and nothing is written.

#### `refuse`

```
refuse(kind, act_key, invocation_id, intent_event_id, actor_ref, credential,
       reason, constituent_code) →
    {refusal_event_id}
  | rejected(
      not-open
    | already-accounted(closing_event_id)
    | recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code)
    )
```

Closes an intent whose bound commit **did not commit** — the constituent refused it on a pre-commit arm (`not-current-custodian`, `already-closed`, a `storage-failure` the constituent declares leaves nothing) — so that the intent does not stand open for the sweep to probe.

Steps, numbered because an earlier round of this page stated the order twice in one paragraph and the two statements disagreed:

0. `not-open`, decided from the invocation's own [Open] result exactly as at [Close] ([Not Open]). Nothing is written.
1. `remaining((kind, act_key), invocation_id)`, exactly as [Close] step 1 and **before any read**. An invocation whose lease expired writes nothing and returns `rejected(recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code))`, the sweep's closing standing in — an abandonment under a declared `commit_fence`, an escalation without one ([Reconcile] step 3), which for a kind with no fence costs an operator's [Resolve] rather than a self-closing act. **A yielded caller reads nothing and adopts nothing at either action**: it is no longer a writer, its read would not be under the section, and the order is what enforces that. Put the read first — as this page did for two rounds — and a lease-expired invocation reports `already-accounted` for a closing it had no standing to observe, where [Close] in the same state reports `recording-failure(outcome)` and adopts nothing.
2. **Re-read the act's records under the section**, exactly as [Close] step 2 and for the same reason. Any closing record naming this `invocation_id` — the sweep writes one for a refusal that never landed, which is precisely this action's failure mode — ends the call with `rejected(already-accounted(closing_event_id))` ([Already Accounted]), nothing appended: the act **is** accounted for and the caller reports it as such, the mirror of [Close] adopting a landed outcome rather than a diagnosis of anything the adopter did wrong. It is deliberately **not** `not-open`, which step 0 owns and which is never decided from a journal read; two meanings on one code here would be worse than elsewhere, because they imply opposite actions — fix the calling code, or report the act as already closed by another writer.
3. Write `<kind>.refused` with `{invocation_id, intent_event_id, kind, act_key, reason, constituent_code}` under the caller's credential — `constituent_code` being the constituent's own pre-commit code, which the adopter has and this composition never sees, passed in so that the refusal record and the `recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code)` arm can both carry it (passed as [Close] takes it). Then clear the map and release the section. The refusal record is the trail's evidence of an authenticated attempt the store refused — a former custodian trying to move a chain they no longer hold — and it is kept, never suppressed. Its `record_action` arms follow the intent position rule: `step-4` and the retention-source `invalid-request` mean the refusal **is** appended — read it back by `invocation_id`, never retry — and `step-2 | step-3`, a lost reply (read back first), `invalid-credential` (a mid-flight revocation), or a non-retention `invalid-request` land `rejected(recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code))` with the intent left open for the sweep. **A refusal that never lands leaves the intent open**, and the sweep's `probe` then finds `not-committed` and writes the abandonment the refusal would have written, without the constituent's `reason`, which died with the invocation; `recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code)` therefore means *the attempt is recorded by the sweep instead* and is never a reason to re-run the act — the second slot carrying the constituent's own code, which the caller has and the sweep does not, so that a caller can tell why the act was refused even though no refusal record says so.

#### `yield`

```
yield(kind, act_key, invocation_id) → ok
```

Releases the section an invocation holds without writing: the adopter's action calls it on its `unknown` partition, so that no adopter touches `act_section` directly. The intent stays open for the sweep.

#### `resolve`

```
resolve(kind, act_key, invocation_id, intent_event_id, actor_ref, credential,
        disposition) →
    {closing_event_id}
  | rejected(
      already-accounted(closing_event_id)
    | not-known
    | section-unavailable
    | invalid-credential
    | invalid-request(purged | malformed | too-young | already-abandoned
                    | candidates-over-cap)
    | recording-failure(resolution)
    )
```

The human-attested close: an operator closes an open intent the sweep cannot — under `service_identity = none`, where the sweep writes nothing, or for an `escalated` act the operator has investigated. The operator runs the adopter's `probe` themselves and supplies the `disposition`: `outcome(outcome_action_ref, outcome_data)`, `abandoned(cause)`, or `escalated(candidates)`; **the operator's credential is validated by the substrate at the closing `record_action`, and nowhere earlier** — a failure is `rejected(invalid-credential)` with the section released and nothing appended, the same arm at the same position [Open] step 4 holds, and the reason this code is in the signature. An earlier round of this action validated it *before the take*, against the substrate's Actor Identity registry directly: **Audit Trail projects no such read** — its six actions are `record_action`, `seal_now`, `read_record`, `verify_record`, `purge_eligible` and `purge_event`, and none of them validates a credential without appending — and *Composes* declares four instance capability requirements, none of them a credential check. A generator reading that clause had no call to make, and both conforming substitutes broke it: calling `record_action` first appends before the section is taken, and calling Actor Identity's `attest` directly mints an undeletable orphan attestation on every operator typo. Validation at the write is where Invariant 1 already puts it. It takes the section (`take((kind, act_key), operator_run_id, closure_latency + journal_write_bound)`, blocking at most the holder's remaining lease; a failed take → `rejected(section-unavailable)`), gates its write on `remaining ≥ journal_write_bound` as [Close] does, re-reads the act's records under it — an outcome or a refusal naming the id → `rejected(already-accounted(closing_event_id))` ([Already Accounted]), the code [Refuse] declares for the identical condition, and **not** `not-open`: this page fixes that code, in three passages, as step 0's single diagnosis, decided from the invocation's own [Open] result and *never from a journal read*, and an earlier round of this action broke that rule here while [Refuse] two sections above was minting a new code precisely to keep from breaking it. One shape, three instances, two rounds — which is why the rule is now stated once, at the code, and every action points at it rather than restating it; **an `invocation_id` with no readable intent record at all → `rejected(not-known)`**, the same code [Read Invocation] returns for an act it cannot find, and deliberately *not* `not-open`: at [Close] and [Refuse] `not-open` means an id this invocation did not open, an adopter's programming error, and reusing it here for an id nobody opened would put two unrelated diagnoses on one code; **an intent whose payload the substrate reports *Purged* → `rejected(invalid-request(purged))`** — read by `read_record(intent_event_id)`, **which is why [Resolve] takes `intent_event_id` as an argument and does not resolve it from `invocation_id`**: every route from an `invocation_id` to an `event_id` on this page reads the intent's *payload* (the filtered range read matches on it, and `open_invocations` is built by that same filter), and the payload is the thing the purge destroyed. Without the id in hand this arm is dead and [Resolve] answers `not-known` for a lawfully destroyed record — the substitution §*Lawful destruction is answered before absence* forbids, and the one [Read Invocation] is allowed to make only because a caller giving it an `act_key` has no id to offer. The operator has one: it is carried by the escalation record they are resolving, and by [Read Invocation] — the cause carried in the code, since an operator must tell lawful destruction, where there is nothing to do, from a malformed disposition, which they can fix and retry — never `not-open`, because absence and lawful destruction are different answers and this page does not let the second read as the first (§*Lawful destruction is answered before absence*) — a purged intent is outside every check and owed no closing record (Invariant 7); **every closing [Resolve] writes over an existing record carries `supersedes = <that record's event_id>`** — an abandonment corrected by an `outcome` disposition (the correction path for a false abandonment, which check 6 reads) and an escalation resolved by any disposition alike. An earlier round required the name for the first and not the second, writing the escalated case with `resolved_by` alone — and `resolved_by` names the **operator**, not a record, so the precedence rule ("replaces the one it names") and check 2's exemption ("together with the one it names") had no referent, and both read a lawful resolution as a `binding_duplicate` on this page's headline recovery path. **The formal model settles it:** the twin with the name omitted for the escalated case is rejected, Invariant 2 and the supersession invariant both violated, against a main model that holds with it; **the disposition against what already stands, in full:** over *nothing*, any disposition writes the act's only closing; over an `escalated` record, any disposition supersedes it by name; over an `abandoned` record, only an `outcome` supersedes it (an `abandoned` over an `abandoned` is `rejected(invalid-request)` — there is nothing to correct, and a second attestation of the same thing is not a correction); over an *outcome* or a *refusal*, nothing supersedes and the call is `already-accounted`. **Every closing [Resolve] writes over an existing record names it in `supersedes`** (§*Which closing stands*, which owns the rule and which the operator's writes must satisfy). The chain the rule exists for is [Resolve]'s own: the escalation is the sweep's, the abandonment and the outcome are two [Resolve] calls, and check 6 is why the second exists. `escalated(candidates)` remains a disposition an operator may supply, so re-escalation is admitted and is superseded by name when it is resolved. **The transitive rule is a prose repair the formal model does not confirm:** the model counts supersessions and does not identify them, so a chain and two records naming one predecessor are the same state to it (Ledger, NOT MODELED). And an `abandoned` disposition for an intent younger than `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance` → `rejected(invalid-request)`, the edge named, since a write may still land — **compared against `now` injected at the operator's own seam** (*Primitive policies*; *Clock semantics*), which is a declared seam of this composition and not a reading the action takes for itself. That is not bookkeeping: this guard is what stops an operator writing `abandoned` while a write of the invocation's may still land, so a reading nothing bounds decides a destructive record. **The formal model reproduces it:** the report-only twin whose operator reading may run one tick ahead violates Invariant 5 — the operator abandons an act whose commit then lands — against a sibling that differs only in that the reading comes from the seam; under `commit_fence = none` the operator's `abandoned` is admitted and is their attestation that the store has been quiet for as long as their judgment requires — writes the closing record **under the operator's own credential** with `recovery = true, resolved_by = actor_ref, acting_actor_ref = <the intent's actor_ref>` and **no `recovery_intended`** (the operator is present and their attestation is the intent), and releases. **Its closing write's `record_action` arms follow the *outcome* position rule, not a nominal one**, because by that point the operator's closing record is the act's account and a second one is the duplicate Invariant 2 forbids: `step-4` and the retention-source `invalid-request` mean the record **is** appended — read it back by `invocation_id`, return success with a hard alert on the unretained record, **never retry**; `step-2 | step-3`, and a lost reply after that read-back comes up empty, land `rejected(recording-failure(resolution))`, which is the one retry-safe arm and means nothing was appended. An earlier round of this page collapsed every journal failure here into `recording-failure(resolution)`, asserted that nothing about the act had changed, and told the operator to retry — which on the `step-4` arm appends a second closing for one `invocation_id`, producing the duplicate in a deployment that *has* `journal_fence` and needs no pause, no lease straddle and no degradation to do it. [Open], [Close], [Refuse] and [Reconcile] each transcribe the position; [Resolve] alone did not, and the Terms card repeated the error, so two sections agreed on it. **`invalid-request` carries the cause, because the five conditions that land it imply three different next moves and an operator cannot guess which:** `malformed` — the disposition is wrong and can be fixed and retried; `candidates-over-cap` — the candidate list exceeds `intent_candidates_cap` and must be narrowed; `purged` — the payload is lawfully destroyed, there is nothing to do, and no retry will ever succeed; `already-abandoned` — an `abandoned` disposition over an already-abandoned intent, which is not a correction and never will be (an `outcome` disposition is the correction path); and `too-young` — **the identical call succeeds once the edge passes**, which is the one case where the right move is to wait and re-issue unchanged. An earlier round declared two members and landed five conditions on them, so an operator waiting out an edge read `malformed` and went hunting for a defect in a well-formed disposition. An operator is a human present with a credential, so no service identity is needed; what the operator writes is their attestation, and Generation acceptance check 6 corroborates it as it corroborates the sweep's.

#### `reconcile`

```
reconcile() →     \* the only action with no `kind`: it sweeps every bound one
    {examined, closed, abandoned, escalated, reported, skipped, closed_already,
     surfaced, unreached}
  | rejected(journal-unavailable)
```

The sweep. Runs at every process restart and on `reconciliation_cadence`; reads `now` once at its own seam. **Where step 1's enumeration itself fails** — the journal unreachable — the run writes nothing, opens or advances the instance finding `journal-unavailable` on `compliance_surface` (*Composition state*, `findings`), and returns `rejected(journal-unavailable)`, the same code [Open] step 3's pre-check returns for the same condition at another position (an earlier round gave the sweep its own name, `enumeration-unavailable`, and two names for one condition is one more than an operator can act on): without this the run would return zero counts and step 5 would surface nothing, since it can only report intents it enumerated, and a journal outage would silently suppress the very at-risk report the window's meetability rests on. **`examined` is defined once and everything else is measured against it: an act step 1 kept and the run *reached* — took, or attempted to take, its section.** An act the run never reached is not examined, whatever stopped it. **The counts then partition it**: `closed + abandoned + escalated + reported + skipped + closed_already = examined`. **Two counts are returned beside the partition and are outside it.** **`surfaced`** counts the unbound-kind intents step 1 reports: they are surfaced and never examined — the run cannot age one, probe it, or bound its pass — so counting them inside `examined` would put an act in the identity that no later count can move, and counting them in `skipped` without counting them in `examined` breaks the identity outright, which is what an earlier round of this page did. **`unreached`** counts the acts step 1 kept that the run stopped short of, which happens for exactly one reason: the service identity's own credential was refused and the run stopped writing. An earlier round put that remainder in `skipped` while `skipped`'s own definition said *examined*, so the identity could not hold on any run that hit it. **`reported`** is the sixth member of the partition and the count a report-only kind's acts land in. **`closed_already`** is the fifth count and the one an earlier round left with no bucket: an act that step 2's re-read finds another writer has closed since step 1 — "release, next" — is neither closed by this run nor left for the next one, and counting it in `skipped` would have said the run had work outstanding when it had none. A run whose `closed_already` is persistently high is racing another run or an operator, which is a deployment fact worth reading off the counts. **`skipped`** carries every act the run examined and genuinely left for the next one, and only those. **Six paths reach it, and the list is exhaustive:** a take that failed; a `probe` that answered `unavailable`; a closing write whose retries the lease cut short; **a closing write left for the next run after step 3 already counted the act** (the count is corrected to `skipped`, since a run reports what it landed, not what it decided); **an act whose `remaining` fell below `journal_write_bound` at step 2** before any write was issued; and **an act the fence refused a write for** while the intent was still short of the abandon edge. Two things are *not* here and were: an unbound kind's intent, which is never examined and is counted in `surfaced`; and the remainder a refused credential leaves, which is never reached and is counted in `unreached`.

  **`reported`** carries every act of a `service_identity = none` kind that the run examined and reported rather than closed. It is a member of the partition because the run did examine it — it took the section and ran `probe` — and it is not `skipped` because no later run will close it either: an operator will, through [Resolve]. An earlier round of this page had no bucket for it at all, so a mixed instance's every run returned an identity that did not hold, and the monitoring rule this page offers on `closed_already` fired on all of them. It is the formal model's fifth component — two runs of it, on two nodes, are the processes the one-writer invariant is proved against — and the one whose discipline the rest of this page exists to state. **With `service_identity = none` — a per-kind binding, so a run over a mixed instance closes the kinds that declare one and reports the kinds that do not, and its counts are the sum over both — it writes nothing for such a kind:** it still takes each section, runs `probe`, and releases — a report of `committed` or `not-committed` needs the probe — and every closing step below becomes a report on `compliance_surface`, which an operator closes through [Resolve]. Such an act is counted in **`reported`**, which is a member of the partition, and never in `skipped`.

Steps:

1. **Enumerate between two edges.** Rebuild `open_invocations` and `act_closings` from the substrate's range read (*Composition state*, the rebuild procedure), and keep the intents whose `recorded_at` is **older than `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance`** (below that the invocation may still hold its lease: a take there would wait on a live act for up to the remaining lease, which the cadence does not budget — the second writer is forbidden by the section and its lease, not by the edge; the formal model holds with the edge removed and skew at zero, and the edge's safety work is the allowance it carries) **and younger than `retention_period`** — written as an inequality because the direction the allowance is applied in decides whether an intent whose closings may already be purge-eligible is examined, and the lower edge names its direction while this one did not: **`recorded_at + retention_period − clock_skew_allowance > now`**, the sweep's reading at its own seam. The instant that inequality names is: the earliest instant any closing record of it could be eligible for purge, whatever the intent's own placement says (an intent placed late reads a later `retention_until`; an intent whose placement failed reads `unresolved` — both are inside this edge exactly as a normally placed one is) (an intent at or past it is outside the sweep whether or not the host has cascaded it yet: its closing records, under the same policy, may already be gone, and a re-close would be the duplicate Invariant 2 forbids; an intent whose payload is destroyed is unreadable to the filter and outside everything — Invariant 7). The same range read carries every **closing** record in the run's delta, whatever its intent's state; the rebuild keeps them for step 5's duplicate scan and discards them with the run. **The same pass surfaces the unbound kinds.** An `<kind>.intended` record whose `<kind>` the *Bindings* table does not serve is a misconfiguration rather than a supported state, so this step reports it on `compliance_surface` and closes nothing: it has no `probe` to run and no `completion_bound` to age against. Two bounds keep the report finite. The upper edge is the **instance's** longest `retention_period`, the kind's own being unknown. And only an intent **no closing record of any kind names** is reported — without that the pass has no way to tell an open intent from one closed long ago, since the membership test that classifies a record as this kind's outcome is itself a per-kind binding, and it would re-report every historical intent of the kind on every cadence. The comparison is the sweep's seam reading against the substrate's stamp, under `clock_skew_allowance`. Under a declared `commit_fence` the abandon edge is one allowance later still — `completion_bound + 2 × clock_skew_allowance` — past which no write of the invocation can land on the store's own clock, so a `not-committed` answer is final; under `none` there is no abandon edge. An intent whose payload the substrate has destroyed fails the filter that builds the map — the sweep cannot read its `invocation_id` and does not try — and is outside the sweep and every check (Invariant 7).
2. **For each such intent, take the act's section** — `take((kind, act_key), run_id, closure_latency + journal_write_bound)`, the run's own seam-injected id as holder, blocking at most the current holder's remaining lease (a holder on a key past the examine edge is an [Open] about to refuse or another run about to close). A take that fails after that wait — the host could not supply the section — skips the act, counts it in `skipped`, and surfaces it in step 5. Under the section, **re-read** `open_invocations[(kind, act_key)]` and `sweep_closed_ids` — an outcome or a closing record naming this `invocation_id` that landed since step 1 means the act is closed: release, count it in `closed_already`, next. Every write below is issued only while `remaining((kind, act_key), run_id)` is at least `journal_write_bound`; below that the run writes nothing more for this act, counts it in `skipped`, and the next run re-reads first — and because every write issued inside a lease lands inside it, the next run's re-read sees everything this run landed.
3. **Probe the store.** Call the adopter's bound `probe(act_key, intent payload)`:
   - `committed(outcome_action_ref, outcome_data)` → **the act happened and its record is owed.** Write `<kind>.recovery_intended` under the service identity, `data = {invocation_id, intent_event_id, act_key, plan = outcome}` — the record that shows the trail the closing was occasioned by the sweep and not by a direct call; it is not itself a closing record, so a run that dies after it writes a second one next run, and check 2 accepts one or more — then the outcome record `outcome_action_ref` under the service identity, `data = outcome_data ∪ {invocation_id, intent_event_id, kind, act_key, recovery = true, acting_actor_ref = <the intent's actor_ref>}`. The outcome's content is what `probe` re-derived from the store, never remembered. Count `closed`.
   - `not-committed` → **the invocation died before its commit, or its refusal record failed** — *if the store can say so.* Where the act kind's `commit_fence` is declared and the intent is past the abandon edge, no write of this invocation can land, the answer is final, and the sweep writes `<kind>.abandoned` under the service identity, `data = {invocation_id, intent_event_id, act_key, cause = not-committed}` — the constituent's refusal reason, where there was one, died with the invocation and the sweep does not guess it; [Resolve] may carry it — nothing is re-run — the act was not done, and re-doing it is the caller's, under a fresh invocation. Count `abandoned`. Where `commit_fence = none`, a write issued before the crash — or by a paused process after it — may still land, `not-committed` is **not final**, and the sweep writes `<kind>.escalated` with `cause = not-observed` instead — the honest degree for a store that offers no fence — and counts `escalated`. Where the fence is declared but the intent is not yet past the abandon edge — between `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance` and `completion_bound + 2 × clock_skew_allowance` — the sweep **writes nothing**, counts the act in `skipped`, and leaves the intent open for the next run.
   - `undecidable(candidates)` → **the store cannot say which.** Write `<kind>.escalated` under the service identity, `data = {invocation_id, intent_event_id, act_key, cause = undecidable, store_candidates = candidates}` — each a reference to a constituent store record, under `reference_length_cap` (at most `intent_candidates_cap`; past it, the count and the range), and surface it on `compliance_surface`. The sweep **never chooses** among candidates and never writes an outcome it did not read (§*Intents pair with outcomes*; §*Recovery commits under a declared service identity*). Count `escalated`.
   - `unavailable` → **the store could not be read.** Write nothing: release, count the act in `skipped`, the intent stays open, the window keeps running, and step 5 surfaces it if the window is at risk. A store outage is never an abandonment. **And the run opens or advances the instance finding `store-unavailable` for this act's `kind`** (*Composition state*, `findings`, which owns the shape, the dedup key `(finding, kind)` and the span's lifecycle) — because Generation acceptance check 3's store branch is keyed on that span and an earlier round of this page keyed it on a record no step produced. The per-act `skipped` count is not that record: it carries no span, and an auditor cannot read an outage's start and end out of a per-act finding.
   Every closing write's `record_action` arms follow the outcome position rule: `step-4` and the retention-source `invalid-request` are landed (read back); `step-2 | step-3` is retried within the section's lease and otherwise left for the next run, the intent still open, the window still running; `invalid-credential` for the service identity itself — a rotated or revoked credential — is a deployment fault the run surfaces on `compliance_surface` at once and writes nothing further under until reconfigured; a non-retention `invalid-request` on a closing write — an outcome that outgrew the adopter's envelope, which the sweep re-derives identically and cannot shrink — closes the act as `<kind>.escalated` with `cause = outcome-unrecordable` (the escalation record is small and fits), so the act is never left unclosable by every writer.
4. Update `open_invocations`, `act_closings` and `sweep_closed_ids`; release the section. All three, because *Composition state* names the sweep among `act_closings`' writers and an earlier round of this step updated two of them.
5. **Liveness, and the duplicate scan.** Any intent this run examined or skipped whose closing has not landed and for which `now − recorded_at` is at least `compensation_window − 2 × run_bound − reconciliation_cadence − clock_skew_allowance` is surfaced on `compliance_surface` *now*, as the act finding **`closure-at-risk`** (*Composition state*, `findings`, which owns its shape and its dedup key `(invocation_id, finding)`) — the sweep itself keeps no marker; the register is what deduplicates, so a standing at-risk act is one record and not one per cadence — so that the breach is reported while the next run can still prevent it. **That threshold is Invariant 4's inequality rearranged, and is derived rather than restated:** subtract from the window every term still ahead of an intent *this* run can already see — this run's own remaining pass, which it may end by dying (`run_bound`), a cadence to the next run, that run's pass (`run_bound`), and the allowance the comparison itself needs — and leave out the terms already spent: `completion_bound`, one allowance, and the whole **in-flight leg** — the inequality's first `run_bound` and first cadence, the run that was already going when the act crossed the edge — since this run has the intent in hand. It is stated here and nowhere else; Invariant 4 and Generation acceptance check 4 carry the inequality it comes from.

   **The duplicate scan, in the same step.** Count closing records by `invocation_id` across the delta step 1 kept, discounting supersession per §*Which closing stands*; **and count OPEN INTENTS by `(kind, act_key)`** — per §*Which closing stands*, whose `service_identity = none` branch this scan reads, so a report-only kind's several open intents are surfaced as the declared degradation and never as `binding_duplicate` — which is the second and less obvious half. Two writers doubling one invocation's records is a duplicate on one id; two *invocations* live on one act is a duplicate on the key, and two invocations carry two ids, so a scan keyed on the id alone cannot see it. That second case is what a fenceless deployment produces (*Composes*, §`journal_fence`), so a scan without it is silent on the commonest duplicate this library's substrate admits — and surface every `invocation_id` with more than one on `compliance_surface` as `binding_duplicate` — the finding [Read Invocation] reports on the read side and Generation acceptance check 2 fails on the audit side, so that the three agree on one name. The scan is over the **delta, not the horizon**: a duplicate is made by a record landing, the substrate stamps `recorded_at` when it lands rather than when it was issued, so every duplicate appears in exactly one run's delta and is reported by that run; a horizon-wide pass would re-report every historical duplicate on every cadence and cost the enumeration the mark exists to avoid. It runs whether or not the kind declares `journal_fence`: with the fence the scan is expected to be silent and a finding is a conformance failure in the fence itself; without it the scan **is** the degraded Invariant 2's detection clause (*Composes*). Return the counts.

**The invocation, a sweep run, and an operator through [Resolve] are the only writers, and the section is what keeps them off one act at once.** The invocation writes while it holds the section and yields at its expiry; the sweep writes only under the section and only for intents past the bound; [Resolve] takes the same section for the same key. Two sweep runs serialize on the section per act and on `sweep_closed_ids` under it. That is Invariant 2, and it is the claim the formal model carries over two sweep runs and an operator racing one invocation across six components.

#### `read_invocation`

```
read_invocation(kind, act_key, invocation_id?) →
    {invocations: sequence of {invocation_id,
                               state: open | closed | refused | abandoned | escalated | resolved,
                               binding_duplicate: true | false,   \* the entry's: two closings on this id
                               records, more},
     binding_duplicate: true | false,   \* the act's: two open intents on this key
     more}
  | rejected(not-known)
  | rejected(journal-unavailable)
```

Reads the act's invocations — each intent and whichever closing record names it — through the same rebuild, **under `now` injected at the reader's own seam** (*Clock semantics*, the fourth): the rebuild's mark advance and its horizon drop are both comparisons against the substrate's stamps, so a read that performs them is a seam like the others and not an exception to the injection rule, one entry per `invocation_id` (a repeatable act has many; a report-only deployment may have several open) — at most `read_cap` (Configuration) of the most recent, with `more = true` where the act has older ones — or one entry where `invocation_id` is given; `records` is the intent and its closing records, **both** the `recovery_intended` records and the closing records among them capped at the most recent `read_cap` with `more = true` beyond it — closings are capped on the same term because the degraded Invariant 2 admits them in plurality, so an act with many duplicates would otherwise return an unbounded list from a read that caps everything else. **Two `binding_duplicate` fields, one per key, and each answers a different question** — which is the shape §*Which closing stands* states and this read implements without restating. The **entry-level** field is the first key: two unsuperseded closings on this `invocation_id`. The **act-level** field, beside `invocations`, is the second key: two open intents on this `(kind, act_key)`, which is a fault of the act and of no single entry, and which under `service_identity = none` is that deployment's declared degradation and is therefore always `false`. For two rounds this read was required by two other sections to report the second key and had nowhere to put it, so a fenceless deployment's commonest duplicate returned a clean act from the one surface an adopter's re-entry arm and an auditor actually call. **The entry's `state` is the standing closing's, read from §*Which closing stands*'s table.** `records` returns every record of the act, superseded ones included, none hidden. Both fields mean exactly what [Reconcile]'s scan and Generation acceptance check 2 mean by them, because all three read the same rule. **`journal-unavailable` ([Journal Unavailable]) is the range read's own `unavailable` arm**, which this read transcribes like every other read path on this page (*Composes*, *Instance capability requirements*): the rebuild it performs is the same rebuild, so an outage is answered as an outage. Without the arm the cheapest conforming implementation answers `not-known` — telling an adopter its act was never opened, for a key whose intent and outcome are both sitting in the journal — which is the absence-for-destruction substitution §*Lawful destruction is answered before absence* forbids two sections away. This read decides nothing and has no `recording-failure` arm to absorb it on, which is why the three deciding positions' argument for omitting the code does not reach it. `not-known` ([Not Known]) is an `act_key` with no readable intent at all — which is also what an act whose every intent the substrate has purged answers, by construction: the destroyed payload carried the key, and this is the one place the composition answers *not known* where the truth is *purged*, named here as §*Lawful destruction is answered before absence*'s admitted exception (the substrate's `read_record` by `event_id` still answers *Purged* for any record an auditor holds an id for). For the adopter's own re-entry arm (*is my act already done?*) and for an auditor. It takes no section and writes nothing; an adopter that decides a write on its answer decides it under the section, through [Open]'s pre-check, not here.

### Which closing stands

**One act may carry more than one closing record, and this is the only place that says which of them is the act's.** Four surfaces need the answer — [Read Invocation] to report a `state`, [Reconcile] step 5 to count duplicates, Generation acceptance check 2 to pass or fail a deployment, and Invariant 2 to mean anything — and for two rounds each of them carried its own statement of the rule. They drifted, as four statements of one rule do: the rule was made transitive at one of them and left pairwise at the other three, and a lawful correction read as a `binding_duplicate` on the page's own recovery path. The rule is stated here and cited there.

**Supersession is by name, and only by name.** A closing record supersedes another by naming it in `supersedes`. `resolved_by` is **not** a supersession trigger: it names the operator who wrote a record, not the record written over, and an earlier round of this page admitted it as one, which left every resolution of an escalation with no referent.

**Supersession is transitive.** The **standing closing** of an act is the one no other closing names, and every record reachable from it through `supersedes` counts with it as **one** closing. The rule must be transitive because chains occur: a sweep escalates, an operator abandons naming the escalation, check 6 shows the abandonment false, and a second [Resolve] writes an outcome naming the abandonment. Stated over pairs, that chain's middle record belongs to two pairs, a pairwise collapse leaves two standing, and the act reads as a duplicate.

**A [Binding Duplicate] is what is left over.** Where two closings each supersede nothing and are superseded by nothing, the act carries `binding_duplicate` and the **lowest `sequence_number`** among them stands; position decides only among unsuperseded records, never against a name, because a superseding record always sits at the higher position. **The second key is the act's**: two *open intents* on one `(kind, act_key)` are also a `binding_duplicate`, reported on the act rather than on an entry, since neither intent is wrong on its own and the fault is that both exist (*Composes*, §`journal_fence`).

**The second key holds ONLY IF the act kind declares a `service_identity`.** Under `service_identity = none` [Open] step 3 is instructed to proceed past an open intent older than the bound and to write its own, so **several open intents on one `(kind, act_key)` are that deployment's declared degradation and are not a duplicate at all** — they are surfaced on `compliance_surface` and closed by an operator through [Resolve]. A report-only deployment that follows this page exactly would otherwise produce, by design and for a whole retention period, the state three surfaces are built to report as a conformance failure; and the reports would be indistinguishable from the fenceless-deployment duplicate the second key was added to catch, which is the signal the second key exists to carry. **The first key is not branched and does not degrade:** two *closings* on one `invocation_id` are a duplicate under every binding, because no report-only instruction on this page produces one. Generation acceptance check 2, [Reconcile] step 5's open-intent count and [Read Invocation]'s act-level field all read this branch here and none of them restates it.

**Which `state` a standing closing projects.** [Read Invocation] returns one of six values per entry and reads them from this table, which is stated here and nowhere else. `resolved` is reserved for the one case where a human's judgement is the only thing that closed an act *and the act did not happen*; an operator's **outcome** is `closed`, because the act did happen and that is what a caller must be told.

```
Standing closing:                          `state`
none                                       open
an outcome record `<kind>.<outcome>`       closed
`<kind>.refused`                           refused
`<kind>.abandoned`, no `resolved_by`       abandoned
`<kind>.abandoned`, with `resolved_by`     resolved
`<kind>.escalated`                         escalated
```

So an `escalated` entry becomes `resolved` when an operator's abandonment names it, and `closed` when any outcome names it. The `abandoned` split is the same one Generation acceptance check 6 reads: it tests the **sweep's** abandonments against `probe` and exempts the operator's, and `resolved_by` is what separates them at both surfaces.

*Cited by:* [Read Invocation]'s `state` and its two `binding_duplicate` fields; [Reconcile] step 5's duplicate scan; Generation acceptance check 2; Invariant 2. None of them restates it.

### The load-bearing wiring decision — one act, one writer, one closer

*Principle.* An irreversible act and its account are two writes no transaction spans. The order — intent, act, outcome — leaves one reachable partial, and closing that partial after the opening process is dead is a second writer by definition. The protocol is sound exactly when the second writer and the first cannot both write for one act, when the second writer cannot mistake in-flight work for a dead invocation's, and when the second writer cannot write what it did not read. All three are properties of the *section*, the *edges*, and the *probe* — and none of them belongs to the adopter's act.

*Likely objection.* Why is this a composition and not a rule on the methodology page that each adopter applies? The rules exist — six of them froze on 2026-08-30 — and twenty adopters applied them, and the closure checks then found the same residue in every one: the section each fix rested on was itself undeclared, its lease semantics unstated, its expiry ambiguous. A rule tells an author what to write; it does not stop twenty authors from writing it twenty ways, and it gives the formal layer twenty models to derive, each of which idealized the leg away. The methodology's *higher-order composition test* names the threshold at which a recurring protocol becomes a compound — five exact instances — and this one clears it several times over — the count of exact instances, the volume of prose re-deriving it, and the closure round that found the identical residue in every copy are the survey tool's output ([`tools/survey/protocol_prose.py`](../tools/survey/protocol_prose.py)) and the methodology's record, not this page's claim.

*Mechanism that resolves it.* The protocol lives here because its invariants are provable only over the pair of processes — invocation and sweep — and the one section they share, and that pair is the same in every adopter. What differs between adopters is exactly the binding set: which store, which key, how long, under whose identity. Those are parameters. An adopter that needs different *behaviour* — a clock-free terminus, a key minted by the commit — declares a deviation, and the deviation is on this page's list or it is a finding against the adopter.

*Result.* One page carries the protocol, one model proves it, one gate attacks it. An adopter's action shrinks to *validate, open, commit, close* with its bindings declared, and the fresh reader who attacks the adopter asks about the adopter's semantics — what the act means, which store is authoritative, what a caller may rely on — rather than hunting lease edges in prose. The measure is the protocol prose per adopter ([`tools/survey/protocol_prose.py`](../tools/survey/protocol_prose.py)) falling to the size of its bindings.

## Composition-level invariants

- **Invariant 1 — Authentication precedes commitment.** No bound `commit` is reached on any invocation before the caller's credential has validated against the substrate's Actor Identity registry for `actor_ref` inside [Open]'s intent record. `invalid-credential` is a pre-state refusal with nothing written anywhere. *Rests on:* Audit Trail's `record_action` and the Actor Identity attestation reached through it (Audit Trail Invariant 1); [Open] step 4 before the adopter's commit. *Defended in-line:* the ordering is [Open] → commit → [Close] in every adopter, and Generation acceptance check 1 tests it from the records alone.

- **Invariant 2 — One writer per act.** For every intent record, at most one **closing** record names its `invocation_id` — an outcome, a refusal, an abandonment, or an escalation, since each of the four closes the act and a second one beside any of them is the duplicate this invariant exists to forbid (the formal model counts them together, and this page previously quantified over outcomes alone, which let an escalation and an outcome coexist in a shape no section of it defined) — and that record was written by exactly one of **three** writers, each under the act's section: the invocation that wrote the intent, while it held the section; a sweep run, under the section, for an intent older than `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance`; or an operator through [Resolve], which takes the same section on the same key for the same reason. Never two of them, and two sweep runs never both close one act. The operator is a writer like the others and is serialized like the others — under `service_identity = none`, where the sweep writes nothing, the operator and a second live invocation of the same key are kept apart by the section alone, which is why [Resolve] takes it rather than trusting the act to be quiet. **With `journal_fence = none` — the case in this library today — this invariant degrades, exactly as Invariant 4 degrades under its own missing bindings, and the degraded form is the one a real deployment meets:** at quiescence at most one closing record names an `invocation_id` save a declared supersession pair, and a late append beside another writer's leaves both records, sets `binding_duplicate`, and is detected, reported and resolved rather than prevented (*Composes*, §`journal_fence`, which states it in full and names the three surfaces that make it checkable). An auditor reading the invariants alone would otherwise read a claim no deployment in this library meets. **Which of an act's closing records is the act's, and when a second one is a duplicate rather than a correction, is §*Which closing stands*** — one rule, cited by every surface that needs it, and stated nowhere else including here. *Rests on:* `act_section` (Configuration) with its lease-as-terminus semantics; the `journal_fence` (Composes) with its margin and its per-write instant, without which the terminus binds nothing a paused holder does and the read-back after a lost reply concludes absence from a disclosed bound; the return-based read-your-writes clause (Composes), without which the handoff at a release outruns the next reader; [Close] step 2's proceed-as-landed; [Reconcile] step 2's re-read under the section and `sweep_closed_ids`. *Defended in-line:* the load-bearing wiring decision; the formal model's **six** rejected twins bearing on this invariant, named as the model names them: `-buggy-death` (the host releases on a holder's death), `-buggy-reread` (the sweep skips its re-read under the section), `-buggy-journal` (`journal_fence = none`), `-buggy-visible` (visibility stated only against time since issue), `-buggy-skew` (the fence instant minted without the margin) and `-buggy-perwrite` (no per-write fence instant) — each of which lands two closings for one act. A seventh, `-buggy-supersede`, lands two *unsuperseded* closings by omitting the name from a resolution written over an escalation, which is the same invariant reached through the operator instead of the sweep.

- **Invariant 3 — Pairing is by the seam-injected key in the journal, and by the bound datum in the store.** Every record this composition writes for an invocation — intent, outcome, refusal, recovery-intended, abandoned, escalated — carries the same `invocation_id`, and every check and every journal join pairs on that field and no other. The one join the journal cannot make — intent to constituent record — is made by `probe` on the act kind's `pairing_datum` by equality, never on a stamp within a window; where that datum is `none` or matches more than one record, the sweep names candidates and closes nothing. *Rests on:* the seam injection (Primitive policies); Event Log Invariant 2 through Audit Trail Invariant 5 (an appended payload is immutable, so the key cannot be rewritten); the `pairing_datum` binding.

- **Invariant 4 — Bounded closure (safety + liveness).** *Safety:* at all times every act whose invocation has yielded or died is detectable from the records alone — an intent older than the bound with no record naming its `invocation_id` — and is never a quiet inconsistency. *Liveness:* **conditional on the journal and the adopter's store being reachable** — an outage leaves the intent open and its key refused `journal-unavailable` at [Open] for the outage's duration, **and is surfaced as an outage rather than as its intents** — [Reconcile] returns `journal-unavailable` at step 1 and never reaches step 5, and step 5 can only report intents it enumerated, so during a journal outage there are no intents to name. The run's outage finding carries the outage's start and end, and that record is what the checks read — **not** `act-in-flight`, which an earlier round promised here and [Open] cannot produce with the journal unreadable, that code's payload being the open intent's `invocation_id` and reading it being the thing that failed — every such intent is closed — an outcome, an abandoned record, or an escalation — within `compensation_window` of its `recorded_at` — **under conditions 1, 4 and 5 of instance start, which this invariant rests on and does not restate** (§*Instance start*, which owns all five and the argument for each; the fifth is here because a `closure_latency` below its parts makes a closure unlandable and no window can be met by a sweep that never closes anything). **With `service_identity = none` the liveness arm degrades to *surfaced*:** every such intent is reported in the register as `closure-at-risk` within the window, and closed by an operator through [Resolve] or by nobody. The run counts such an act in `reported`, which is a member of its count partition and not in `skipped` — no later run will close it either. **With `commit_fence = none` the abandoned arm degrades to *escalated*:** an act the store cannot certify as not-committed is surfaced, never abandoned. *Rests on:* [Reconcile]'s edges and steps 3–5; the act kind's `completion_bound` and `retention_period`, and the instance's `clock_skew_allowance`, `reconciliation_cadence`, `run_bound`, `closure_latency`, `read_bound`, `journal_write_bound` and `compensation_window`; the substrate's range read.

- **Invariant 5 — Recovery is derived, never remembered.** *Three clauses, split by writer, because the writers owe different things.* **(a) The sweep's outcomes:** every outcome the sweep writes is preceded by one or more `<kind>.recovery_intended` records naming the act and the plan, is attested under the declared service identity with the original caller in `acting_actor_ref`, and carries only what the adopter's `probe` re-derived from the store. **(b) An operator's outcomes, through [Resolve]:** attested under the operator's own credential with the original caller in `acting_actor_ref`, carrying `resolved_by`, and **owing no `recovery_intended`** — the operator is present and their attestation is the intent, which is the shape Generation acceptance check 2 admits as (b) and which an earlier round of this invariant obliged to carry a record check 2 says is not owed. **(c) Abandonments, whoever writes them:** an `<kind>.abandoned` record asserts that the act did not happen, so it is written only where the store can say so — the sweep writes one only for an act kind with a declared `commit_fence` and only past the abandon edge, and an operator writes one only past the too-young edge and as their own attestation. This is the clause Generation acceptance check 6 and [Resolve]'s too-young guard both rest on, and for two rounds neither had text here to rest on. Under all three: what the store cannot re-derive is escalated with candidates, not written; what the store cannot read is not closed at all. *Rests on:* `service_identity` (Bindings); Audit Trail Invariant 1 (the attesting actor is verified); [Reconcile] step 3.

- **Invariant 6 — The outcome is sized before the intent.** No act commits whose outcome record, or whose compensation record, could be refused by the substrate's `payload_cap` — conditional on the adopter's `outcome_envelope` being the true maximum, a binding obligation the externally-clearable checks name; [Open] sizes both against `outcome_envelope` and `intent_candidates_cap` before the intent lands, and refuses `invalid-request` with nothing written. *Rests on:* [Open] step 1; the adopter's caps under `reference_length_cap`.

- **Invariant 7 — The sweep is bounded at both ends.** [Reconcile] examines no intent younger than `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance` and none for which `recorded_at + retention_period − clock_skew_allowance > now` fails at the sweep's own seam ([Reconcile] step 1, which owns both edges and the direction each applies the allowance in); past that edge it closes nothing, and an intent whose payload the substrate has destroyed is outside every check. *Rests on:* Audit Trail Invariant 2 (retention coverage at quiescence — every `<kind>.*` record is placed under the kind's fixed policy, late at worst), Audit Trail Invariant 4 (the cascade destroys the payload), Audit Trail Invariant 8 and its `read_record` step 5 (destruction and an unplaced retention are each honestly reported), and the `journal` binding's fixed-policy obligation.

- **Invariant 8 — Positioned codes.** Every code an adopter's action exports that could land before its commit and after it carries the position — `recording-failure(intent | outcome)` at least — and the adopter's signature declares the payload; a caller who receives `intent` may retry the action, a caller who receives `outcome` must not, because the act exists. *Rests on:* [Open], [Close], and [Refuse]'s signatures; the position rule (Primitive policies).

- **Invariant 9 — Constituent invariants preserved.** All Audit Trail invariants (1–8) hold over the journal instance, and transitively those of its four constituents. This composition writes nothing to the adopter's constituent store and weakens no constituent invariant by paraphrase: the adopter's `commit` partition transcribes the constituent's arms as the constituent states them.

## Examples

### Walkthrough — a disclosure, three ways

The adopter is Immutable Transaction Ledger's `disclose_subset`, bound as: `act_key = disclosure of (subject_ref, recipient, scope, authority) by actor_ref`; `commit = SelectiveDisclosure.record(subject_ref, recipient, scope, authority, disclosed_at = now)`, whose `invalid-request`, `unknown-authority-type`, and `storage-failure` are pre-commit as the constituent declares them, and whose lost reply is `unknown`; `pairing_datum = disclosed_at`, the invocation's seam-injected `now`, carried in `intent_data` and passed into `commit`, so the store's record carries the same value by construction — and, since this kind is `repeatable = yes` and Selective Disclosure admits no nonce field to fall back on, the binding **declares the obligation that choice incurs**: no two serialized invocations of one `act_key` may carry the same reading on any node of the instance, which this deployment discharges by minting `disclosed_at` from a per-node monotonic source whose low bits carry the node, not from a bare wall-clock read — **and the tag is placed below the resolution at which the wired instance's guard can discriminate** — Selective Disclosure's not-in-future check is a comparison against the reading its own pipeline injects, and this deployment's obligation is that the two seams share a clock authority, so what must hold is a property of the wiring and not of the atom: the tag occupies bits finer than the shared authority's tick, so a tagged stamp is never *ahead* of a reading taken from that authority and the guard refuses nothing a conforming adopter issues. A deployment whose authority ticks at or below the tag's resolution must tag elsewhere. A deployment whose constituent compares at the resolution the tag occupies must tag elsewhere or accept the refusals, which are pre-commit and safe; `probe` = read Selective Disclosure's store by `subject_ref` and `recipient` (the filters its `read` declares) and select, in the adopter's code, the record whose `scope`, authority, and `disclosed_at` equal the intent's, answering `committed(ledger.disclosed, {disclosure_id, disclosed_at})` on exactly one match, `not-committed` on none, `undecidable(candidates)` on several, and `unavailable` on any read outage — the constituent's `invalid-query`, a programming fault, is `unavailable` to the sweep *and* surfaced on `compliance_surface`; and the adopter's seam and Selective Disclosure's seam share one clock authority, a declared deployment obligation, so that the constituent's not-in-future guard on `disclosed_at` (run against its own reading) refuses nothing a conforming adopter issues — and where it does refuse, the arm is pre-commit and safe; `commit_fence = none` (Selective Disclosure's `record` carries no deadline and the deployment declares no store-level fence, so this kind is escalated, never abandoned, by the sweep); `repeatable = yes`; `completion_bound = 30 s`; `service_identity = ledger-reconciler`; `reconciliation_cadence = 60 s`; `clock_skew_allowance = 2 s`; `closure_latency = 12 s`; `run_bound = 135 s` (a backlog of **three** orphans, sequentially, each costing at worst one holder's remaining lease and one closure — `3 × (30 + 12) = 126 s`, with the balance as headroom; an earlier round wrote twenty-four closures at `closure_latency` and left nothing for the waits `run_bound`'s own definition requires, and a deployment sizing for twenty-four orphans a run must disclose the sum and carry a window to match); `journal_write_bound = 3 s`; `read_bound = 2 s`; `compensation_window = 10 min`.

**All five conditions of instance start, shown against these numbers** (§*Instance start* states them; this is the arithmetic, not a second statement of the rule):

1. `30 + 2×2 + 2×60 + 3×135 = 559 s < 600 s`.
2. `30 + 2 + 600 = 632 s < retention_period`, which needs a `retention_period` **above 632 s**, which `ledger.*` has.
3. `30 s > 2×2 + 2×3 + 1 = 11 s` — two reads, two writes and the ledger commit's round-trip. Neither fence is declared here, so no allowance term is charged.
4. `135 s ≥ max(30, 12 + 3) + 12 = 42 s`.
5. `12 s > 2 + 2×3 + 1 = 9 s`.

The sweep's lease is `12 + 3 = 15 s`, and one closure inside it spends `2 + 1 + 3 + 3 = 9 s`, clearing the `remaining ≥ journal_write_bound` gate before both of its writes. An earlier round of this walkthrough bound `closure_latency = 5 s`, which breaches condition 5 and under which the second gate fails for any probe latency at all, so the sweep re-opened a recovery intent every cadence and never landed a closing. The instance starts.

**The clean run.** `disclose_subset(...)` validates its inputs; [Open] takes the section on the act, sizes the outcome (`disclosed_entry_ids` under the ledger's cardinality cap) and the compensation, writes `ledger.disclose_intended` (`inv-7f2`) under the discloser's credential — verified there — and returns. `SelectiveDisclosure.record` → `dsc-411`. [Close] finds no outcome for `inv-7f2` under the section, writes `ledger.disclosed` with `{invocation_id: inv-7f2, intent_event_id, disclosure_id: dsc-411, ...}`, releases, returns `landed_by = invocation`. One intent, one outcome, one writer.

**The crash.** The same run dies between `SelectiveDisclosure.record` returning `dsc-411` and [Close]. The section stays held until its lease runs out at `t + 30 s`; the host does not see the death. The intent stands open. Thirty-two seconds later a [Reconcile] run keeps `inv-7f2` (older than `30 + 2 s`, inside the horizon), tries the section, re-reads — still open — and probes: Selective Disclosure holds exactly one record matching the four fields at the invocation's instant → `committed(ledger.disclosed, {disclosure_id: dsc-411, disclosed_at})`. The sweep writes `ledger.recovery_intended` then `ledger.disclosed` with `recovery = true, acting_actor_ref = <the discloser>`, both under `ledger-reconciler`, and releases. The disclosure is accounted for within about forty-five seconds of being made — the bound, the allowance, the wait for the run, and a closure at `closure_latency` — inside the ten-minute window, by the one writer left alive. The formal model's second process, exercised.

**The stall.** The same run does not die; its `SelectiveDisclosure.record` is slow. Two orders are possible, and both are safe. The store applies the write at eight seconds and the invocation, slow for some other reason, reaches [Close] at forty: `remaining` answers `none` — the lease expired at thirty — so the invocation has yielded, writes nothing, and returns `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))`; the caller reads the position (*the act exists; do not re-run*); the sweep at thirty-five probed `committed` and closed it, or will. Or the store never applied the write: the commit is refused, the adopter calls [Refuse], and if that too failed, the sweep's `probe` answers `not-committed` — which, under this kind's `commit_fence = none`, is **not final**: a process that paused past the lease could still issue the write at forty-five and the store, fenceless, would apply it. So the sweep writes `escalated` with `cause = not-observed`, the operator investigates, and the operator — not the sweep — writes the abandonment through [Resolve] once the store has been quiet for as long as their judgment requires. The third order — a write landing at fifty after a sweep at thirty-five has read the store empty — is therefore survivable: the escalation stands, the operator's later `probe` finds the record, and their disposition is `outcome`, not `abandoned`. A deployment that declares a store-level fence for this kind gets `abandoned` from the sweep instead, and the third order becomes unreachable on the store's own clock.

**The refusal.** A different caller opens a disclosure and `SelectiveDisclosure.record` refuses `invalid-request` (a scope the constituent rejects). The adopter partitions that arm as pre-commit and calls [Refuse]; `ledger.disclosure_refused` lands with the reason; the intent is closed; the trail keeps the authenticated attempt. Nothing for the sweep.

### Rejection path — a second invocation of the same act

Two operators call `transfer_custody` for one chain within a second of each other, in an adopter whose `act_key` is the chain. The first's [Open] takes the section and writes its intent. The second's [Open] waits on the section, takes it after the first's [Close] releases, and its step-3 pre-check finds no open intent — the outcome read runs only for a `repeatable = no` kind, and a transfer is repeatable against a chain — so it proceeds to write its own intent and its own transfer. Had the act kind been non-repeatable (a genesis), the second would have landed `rejected(act-landed(outcome_event_id))` and the adopter's `already-*` arm would have answered its caller.

### Rejection path — the caller's retry after `outcome`

A caller receives `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` from an adopter's action. The position tells it the act committed. It does not re-run the action; it calls the adopter's read (through [Read Invocation]) and finds the act `open` — the sweep has not yet run — then, a minute later, `closed` with `recovery = true`. A caller that re-ran the action instead would have found `rejected(act-in-flight(invocation_id))` at [Open], because the open intent is younger than the bound plus the allowance — and, after the sweep, an `act-landed` refusal where the act is non-repeatable, or a second act where it is: the second act is the caller's decision, made with the position in hand.

### Regulated adversarial scenarios

**Regulator audit — "show me that every consequential act you took is accounted for."** The auditor enumerates the journal's `<kind>.intended` records inside the horizon and older than the bound, and for each finds exactly one **closing** record naming its `invocation_id` — an outcome, a refusal, an abandoned, or an escalation — in each case not since superseded by a resolution, an `abandoned` carrying a later `outcome` with `supersedes` being the corrected-false-abandonment shape check 6 reads, and leaving two records where the closing is the later one; `recovery_intended` records are not closings and may be several — within `compensation_window` of its `recorded_at`, under the allowance (Generation acceptance checks 2 and 3). The invariant the query rests on is Invariant 4; the structural answer for an intent with no closing record is that it is younger than the bound (in flight), or it is a conformance failure surfaced on `compliance_surface`, and the auditor can tell which from the stamps alone.

**Disputed act — "I never authorized that disclosure."** The outcome record names `invocation_id`; the intent record with that id is attested under the disputant's credential, verified by Actor Identity before the store committed (Invariant 1), and sealed (Audit Trail Invariant 3). If the outcome carries `recovery = true`, the sweep wrote it — under `ledger-reconciler`, behind a `recovery_intended` record naming the plan — and `acting_actor_ref` still names the disputant, whose intent record still stands under their own credential. The structural rebuttal is the intent, not the outcome; the sweep changes who attests the outcome, never who attested the intent.

**Breach investigation — "the reconciler's credential was compromised; what could it have written?"** Every record under the service identity is a closing record naming an `invocation_id` whose intent stands under a human's credential; an outcome under the service identity with no such intent is a write outside this composition (Generation acceptance check 5), and an outcome whose content the adopter's store does not corroborate is one the sweep could not have derived (check 6). The forensic window is bounded: the compromised identity could close acts, never open them, and could close only acts whose intents exist — the intent record is the control the service identity cannot forge.

## Generation acceptance

A derived implementation of Recoverable Invocation is acceptable when an auditor, given the journal and the adopter's constituent store, can clear the following from the records alone.

### Audit-Trail-traversal-clearable checks

1. **Authentication precedes commitment.** For every outcome record **whose intent is inside the horizon** — decided by `read_record(intent_event_id)` answering with a retention state of *Retained*, not by arithmetic on the outcome's own stamp and not by the mere presence of an answer — `read_record` still answers for a purged intent, with the payload destroyed, and that answer routes to the purged case this same sentence names — because [Resolve] puts no bound between an intent and the outcome that closes it and an operator may resolve long after the intent's period has run — an intent record with the same `invocation_id` precedes it in the journal's sequence, attested under the actor the outcome names: its own `actor_ref` where `recovery` is absent, its `acting_actor_ref` where `recovery = true`. An outcome with no preceding readable intent is a conformance failure; an outcome whose intent the substrate reports *Purged* is answered *purged*, never *absent* (§*Lawful destruction is answered before absence*). Invariant 1.
2. **One closing per intent, one intent per invocation, one writer.** For every intent record inside the horizon and older than `completion_bound + clock_skew_allowance`, at most one **closing** record of any of the four kinds — an outcome, a refusal, an abandonment, or an escalation — names its `invocation_id`, **at most one `<kind>.intended` record does either**, and — the second key, **as §*Which closing stands* states it, including its `service_identity = none` branch, which this check reads and does not restate** — **at most one intent per `(kind, act_key)` is open at any reading inside the horizon**, an intent being open until a closing names it. Two of either is a conformance failure (`binding_duplicate`). Quantified over outcomes alone, as an earlier round wrote it, this check reads an escalation beside an outcome as clean, which is the commonest duplicate a fenceless kind produces; and quantified over closings alone it misses the second intent the same lost-reply retry can append at [Open] step 4 (*Composes*, §`journal_fence`). **The exemption is supersession, read transitively per §*Which closing stands*:** a standing closing and everything reachable from it through `supersedes` count as one, which is the operator correcting an escalation or an abandonment rather than two writers disagreeing. Where the closing is an outcome it is one of three shapes, and the auditor reads which from its payload: `recovery` absent — its `actor_ref` equals the intent's; `recovery = true` with `resolved_by` — written through [Resolve], its `actor_ref` equals `resolved_by`, an operator, and no `recovery_intended` is owed; `recovery = true` without `resolved_by` — the sweep's, its `actor_ref` the service identity, and one or more `<kind>.recovery_intended` naming the same `invocation_id` precede it. Any other shape is a conformance failure. Invariants 2 and 5.
3. **Bounded closure** *(both branches externally clearable: the `service_identity = none` branch and the outage exemption each read `compliance_surface`, which leaves no journal record)*. For every such intent, a closing record — outcome, refusal, abandoned, or escalated — lands within `compensation_window` of the intent's `recorded_at`, the auditor's own reading tolerating `clock_skew_allowance` across seams. An intent with none past that is a conformance failure — except one of a kind the `bindings` table no longer serves, and one **whose window overlaps a declared outage of the journal or the adopter's store**, which is the suspension Invariant 4's liveness arm declares. The exemption is keyed on an **instance finding's span** (*Composition state*, `findings`, which owns both names and the span's lifecycle) and not on the intent: **`journal-unavailable`**, which [Reconcile] step 1 opens when its enumeration fails and which carries no `kind` because the journal is the instance's, and **`store-unavailable`**, which step 3 opens when `probe` answers `unavailable` and which carries **the kind whose store it was** — one adopter's outage exempts that kind's intents and no others. An intent is exempt if its window overlaps **any span of either condition that applies to its kind**; a condition that ceased and recurred has two spans and the healthy gap between them exempts nothing. It is not keyed on the intent because during a journal outage no intent is enumerable, so per-intent evidence is something the composition structurally cannot produce; an earlier round asked the auditor for exactly that evidence, and a later one keyed the store branch on a record no step produced — both would have read lawfully-handled orphans as conformance failures. A store outage's intents do also reach step 5 once they cross the at-risk threshold, which is a second and weaker record of the same condition and is not what clears the check; one younger than the bound is in flight and not counted. Under `service_identity = none`, the check is instead that every such intent appears in the register as a **`closure-at-risk`** act finding within the window (externally clearable, since a surfacing leaves no journal record). Invariant 4.
4. **The inequality holds.** For every bound act kind, read the kind's `completion_bound` and `retention_period` and the instance's `clock_skew_allowance`, `reconciliation_cadence`, `run_bound`, `closure_latency`, `read_bound`, `journal_write_bound` and `compensation_window` — named rather than counted, since the conditions do not draw on the same set — and confirm **all five** conditions of instance start as §*Instance start* states them, four of them strict. This check enumerates none of them: for two rounds it named a subset, and the condition it left out is the one the page's own walkthrough breached. Clearing this check is clearing instance start. Invariant 4 for conditions 1, 4 and 5, Invariants 4 and 7 for condition 2, and Invariant 4's meetability for condition 3.
5. **The service identity only closes.** Every record attested by the service identity whose named intent is inside the horizon (as check 1 bounds it) names an `invocation_id` whose intent record exists and is attested by a different actor; a service-identity record naming no readable intent inside that bound is a write outside this composition, and one whose intent is *Purged* is answered *purged*. Invariant 5.
6. **The sweep wrote only what it read.** For every outcome carrying `recovery = true`, the adopter's `probe` — run by the auditor now against the constituent store — corroborates its `outcome_data` (or answers `unavailable`, which is *not applicable*, never failed); for every `<kind>.abandoned` not superseded by an outcome carrying `supersedes`, `probe` now answers `not-committed` — or `undecidable` where the act kind's `pairing_datum` is `none` and a later identical act exists — and, where the record carries no `resolved_by` (the sweep's), the act kind's `commit_fence` is declared; an `<kind>.escalated` record's `store_candidates` are each a store record matching the intent. Invariant 5.
7. **Horizon.** Checks 1–3 quantify over intents whose payload is readable — inside the journal's retention horizon; an intent the substrate reports Purged is outside every check and owed no record. Invariant 7.
8. **Constituent bars.** Audit Trail's eight traversal checks over the journal instance, per its own Generation acceptance section.

### Externally-clearable checks

- Whether a **declared outage** of the journal or the adopter's store was itself surfaced on `compliance_surface` with its start and its end, and whether every intent whose window overlapped it was closed once the outage ended — the exemption Generation acceptance check 3 grants, which leaves no journal record and so can be cleared only here. The clearance is keyed on the **outage record**, not on the intents: during a journal outage nothing is enumerable, so an auditor who asks whether each intent was surfaced is asking for evidence the composition cannot produce, and would read lawful behaviour as a breach.
- Whether `act_section` is in fact shared across every node of the instance and implemented as a lease of the declared length — the records show its consequence (no `binding_duplicate` at quiescence), not the mechanism.
- Whether `closure_latency`, `completion_bound`, `read_bound` and `run_bound` were set from observed worst cases with headroom — `run_bound` in particular over **the largest backlog the deployment sizes for**, which no record can show, since a run that never met that backlog is indistinguishable from one whose bound was right; the records show breaches, not the setting rule.
- Whether the adopter's `probe` is complete — whether the store it reads is the store the act was made in. A `probe` that reads a projection can answer `not-committed` for a committed act, and the abandoned record it causes is false; only the adopter's own constituent contract can clear this.
- Under `service_identity = none`, whether the surfaced intents were acted on through [Resolve]; the composition closes nothing on its own and the deployment's governance owns the rest.
- Whether `commit_fence` is honoured by the store — that a write carrying the deadline §*Where the allowance goes* mints for it (`expires_at − clock_skew_allowance`, not the bare expiry) is never applied after it; the records show the consequence (no `abandoned` record whose act the store later holds), not the mechanism.
- Whether each adopter's `outcome_envelope` is the true maximum of the records its act kind writes; an outcome that outgrows it lands `recording-failure(outcome)` and is the sweep's to write from `probe`.
- Whether any writer other than this composition's actions uses an adopter's `journal` vocabulary (`<kind>.intended`, `<kind>.<outcome>`, and the rest); the substrate does not reserve namespaces, so this is a deployment's discipline to show.

## Edge cases and explicit non-goals

- **Cross-store consistency under partial failure.** The composition's whole subject, stated once more as a list of the reachable partials and their closers. *Crash after [Open], before the commit:* intent open, store untouched; the sweep's `probe` answers `not-committed` and it writes `<kind>.abandoned` where the act kind declares a `commit_fence` and the intent is past the abandon edge, `<kind>.escalated` otherwise — the fenceless case being the common one, since no atom in this library declares a fence today. *Crash after the commit, before [Close]:* the store holds the act, the intent is open; `probe` answers `committed`, the sweep writes the outcome under `recovery = true`. *Crash inside [Close] after the outcome landed and before the map update:* the map is a rebuild trigger, not a partial; the next read rebuilds. *A stalled invocation past its lease:* it yields; the sweep closes; the invocation's late [Close] adopts or reports `outcome`. *A refusal record that failed:* the intent stands, `probe` answers `not-committed`, and the sweep closes it — abandoned under a fence, escalated without one. *An outcome the store cannot corroborate:* `undecidable`, escalated with candidates, never chosen. There is no sixth partial, and a deployment that finds one has a section that is not a section.

- **Declared deviations.** An adopter whose act does not fit the common contract declares one of the following in its bindings, and no other; a need outside this list is a finding against the adopter, or evidence for the next revision of this page — recorded first, per the methodology's freeze discipline. *(a) `retry_terminus = counted(n)`:* an adopter that bounds its retry without a clock (Chain of Custody, which still carries the seam-injected `now` [Open] step 3 needs) bounds its in-invocation *retry* of the outcome by `n` attempts; [Close]'s `remaining` query before every write is unchanged, so the lease still governs when the invocation may write and the sweep's side is untouched. *(b) Minted key:* an act whose `act_key` is produced by the commit (a genesis chain, an allocation) binds the key it can name before the commit and declares the `probe` that finds the minted record from it — where no such read exists in the constituent, the adopter declares `probe = undecidable` for that kind and the sweep escalates every orphan of it, which is the honest liveness degree. *(c) Journal without attribution:* an adopter journaling to a bare Event Log rather than Audit Trail is not an adopter of this page — it is the second group the higher-order composition test names, one instance short of its own compound — and says so.

- **Repeatable and non-repeatable acts.** Whether two invocations against one `act_key` are two acts (a transfer, a review) or one act attempted twice (a genesis, a close) is the adopter's declaration; [Open]'s `act-landed` refusal fires only for the latter. The composition does not guess.

- **What the sweep never does.** It never re-runs an act (a re-run is the caller's, under a fresh invocation, through the adopter's own re-entry arm) — the only adopter code it runs is `probe`, as the section's holder; it never chooses among candidates; it never writes under a human's credential; it never abandons an act a store outage, a missing fence, or an unexpired abandon edge leaves uncertain; it never examines an intent younger than the bound or older than the horizon; it never waits longer than a holder's remaining lease for a section. A sweep described as doing any of these is not this composition's.

- **Clock semantics.** The composition samples no clock: `now` and `invocation_id` are injected at the adopter's seam per invocation, at the sweep's seam once per run, at **the operator's seam once per [Resolve] call** alongside `operator_run_id`, and at **the reader's seam once per [Read Invocation] call**, which performs the same rebuild and therefore the same comparisons. [Resolve] is a writer like the other two and reads its clock the way the other two read theirs; an implementation that reaches its too-young guard with no injected reading in scope and closes the step with a clock read inside the action has broken this rule, and the formal model's report-only twin shows what that costs (Invariant 5: an act abandoned whose commit then lands). Every stamp on a record is the substrate's (`recorded_at`, at its seam) or the adopter's constituent's (returned by `probe`); a comparison across seams runs under `clock_skew_allowance` and only excludes, never decides. The lease is timed by the host, not by this composition.

- **Not a transaction.** No adopter may describe [Open] → commit → [Close] as atomic, and this composition offers no rollback of anything; an adopter that needs a withdrawable act composes a Transaction pattern *(forthcoming)* over a store that offers one.

- **Not the audit journal.** Attribution, sealing, retention, purge, and the range read are Audit Trail's, consumed as declared; this composition adds record kinds to the journal and nothing to its contract. The substrate accepts a `<kind>.*` record from any authenticated caller, so *one outcome per intent* is enforced by this composition's writers and by nobody else: that no other writer uses an adopter's `journal` vocabulary is an externally-clearable check, listed under Generation acceptance.

- **Not the adopter's re-entry.** An adopter's `already-*` arm — what its action does when its caller re-runs an act that is done — is the adopter's, informed by [Read Invocation]; this composition refuses the second *intent* for a non-repeatable act and leaves the answer to the caller to the adopter.

- **Retention of this composition's records.** The intent, outcome, refusal, and closing records live under the journal's `retention_policy` like every other record there; a deployment whose regulator requires the account to outlive the act's own retention sets the journal's policy accordingly. The adopter's constituent store has its own lifecycle, which this composition does not govern; an act whose store record is purged before its journal records is corroborated by nothing (check 6 reads *not applicable*, never *failed*).

## Terms

The canonical concepts this spec refers to. Each `[Term]` marker in the prose above links to its card here. A card states what the concept *is*, in plain English, plus its **Kind** — one of four: **Type** (a thing or category), **Operation** (a behavior), **Member** (a value of an enumerated Type), or, for a named datum, **Field** (a datum a Type carries) or **Parameter** (a value an Operation needs). This is a composition, so its own concepts are its six actions and one read, and the records and outcomes they produce; the substrate's rejections (`invalid-credential`, `invalid-request`, `recording-failure(step)`) are relayed at their declared positions and are the substrate's cards, not this page's.

#### Open

The composition action an adopter's action calls before its bound commit: takes the act's section, sizes the outcome and the compensation against the substrate's cap, refuses an act already in flight or already landed, and writes the intent record under the caller's credential — where authentication precedes commitment. Returns `{invocation_id, intent_event_id}` with the section held.

Kind: Operation

#### Close

The composition action an adopter's action calls after its bound commit returned: under the section, adopts an outcome the sweep already landed for this invocation or writes the outcome record, then releases. Returns `landed_by: invocation | sweep | operator`; returns `recording-failure(outcome)` where the lease expired or the record could not land — the act exists and the sweep will close it.

Kind: Operation

#### Refuse

The composition action an adopter's action calls when its bound commit refused on a pre-commit arm: writes the refusal record naming the intent, so the authenticated attempt is kept and the intent does not stand open, and releases the section.

Kind: Operation

#### Reconcile

The sweep: the composition's second writer, run at restart and on cadence, examining open intents between the completion bound and the retention horizon, taking each act's section, probing the adopter's store, and closing each act with an outcome it re-derived, an abandonment, or an escalation naming candidates — under the service identity, behind a recovery-intended record, or as a report only where no identity is declared.

Kind: Operation

#### Yield

The release an adopter's action calls on a lost commit reply: the section is released, nothing is written, the intent stays open for the sweep.

Kind: Operation

#### Resolve

The human-attested close: an operator, present with their own credential, closes an open intent the sweep cannot — under a report-only deployment, or after investigating an escalation — supplying the disposition their own run of the adopter's `probe` supports; the record carries `recovery = true` and `resolved_by`.

Kind: Operation

#### Read Invocation

The composition's read: the act's invocations, each with its records and its state (`open | closed | refused | abandoned | escalated | resolved`), for an adopter's re-entry arm and for an auditor. Takes no section, writes nothing.

Kind: Operation

#### Recording Failure

The composition's positioned failure code, `recording-failure(intent | outcome | refusal | resolution)`, the `refusal` position carrying a second slot for the constituent's own pre-commit code (`recording-failure(refusal, constituent_code)`) and the others none: `intent` means nothing committed and the whole action may be retried; `outcome` means the act exists or may exist, its record is the sweep's (or an operator's, through [Resolve]), and the caller must not re-run the action; `refusal` means the refusal record did not land, the intent stays open, and the sweep — or, in a report-only deployment, an operator — will close it: an abandonment where the act kind declares a `commit_fence`, an escalation where it does not, since without a fence `not-committed` is not final ([Reconcile] step 3); `resolution` means an operator's closing record did not land and nothing changed — the retry-safe arm, and *only* the retry-safe arm: at [Resolve], as everywhere else, `step-4` and the retention-source `invalid-request` mean the record **is** appended and is read back rather than retried, since a retry there appends the second closing Invariant 2 forbids.

Kind:      Type
Role:      the positioned failure code
Projects:  recording_failure

#### Act In Flight

[Open]'s refusal where an open intent for the same act exists — younger than the bound (another invocation is between its intent and its outcome) or older (a dead invocation the sweep will close within the window). Carries the open `invocation_id`.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the open rejections
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  act_in_flight

#### Section Unavailable

[Open]'s and [Resolve]'s refusal where the act's section could not be taken within the current holder's remaining lease; nothing is written and the call may be retried.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the open rejections and the resolve rejections
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  section_unavailable

#### Not Open

[Close]'s and [Refuse]'s step 0 refusal, and theirs alone: an `invocation_id` and `intent_event_id` pair the adopter's action did not receive from its own [Open]. It is an adopter's programming error, not a protocol state, and nothing is written.

**It is a precondition on the caller, and this composition does not decide it.** The name is exported so that an adopter has one code for the condition; the *check* runs in the adopter's action, which holds [Open]'s returned pair on its own call stack for the life of the invocation — ephemeral local state within a single action invocation, which is exactly where [`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Logic Confinement Principle puts it. Both journal-derived sources are foreclosed and for one reason: on a multi-node instance a local absence is not a miss, so a map or a range read would answer `not-open` for a live invocation another node opened a moment ago. **Nothing else this composition retains could answer it**, and retaining a per-invocation handle would be the composition-owned state the execution contract forbids.

**What happens when a caller does not enforce it is named here rather than left to be found.** A `close` or `refuse` carrying a pair no [Open] returned reaches step 1, `remaining` answers `none` for a non-holder, and the call lands `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` — *the act may exist; do not re-run* — for an act that was never opened. That is a **false positive in the safe direction and not an inconsistency**: no intent record exists, so no sweep run, no check and no operator will ever look at the id, and nothing is left open. It is the cost of the foreclosure above, and it is the caller's to avoid. The condition "another writer already closed this act" is [Already Accounted] at [Refuse] and at [Resolve], and adopting a landed outcome is [Close] step 2's business; three rounds of this page tried to land one of those on this code and the rule is stated here so that a fourth does not.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the close rejections and the refuse rejections
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  not_open

#### Not Known

The read's answer for an act with no readable intent at all, at [Read Invocation] and at [Resolve]. It is **absence**, not destruction: a lawfully purged intent is answered `invalid-request(purged)` at [Resolve], which takes `intent_event_id` for exactly that reason. [Read Invocation] is the one surface that cannot make the distinction and says so — a caller giving it an `act_key` has no id to offer, and the destroyed payload carried the key.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the read rejections and the resolve rejections
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  not_known

#### Finding

A record in this instance's `findings` register (*Composition state*, which owns the contract: the two shapes, the two dedup keys, the span's lifecycle, the durability floor, the per-`act_key` order, and the five named conditions no surface may read past). The surface it lands on is *Configuration*'s `compliance_surface`. An **act finding** names one act the run could not close and carries no span; an **instance finding** names a condition of the instance, or of one bound kind, and carries one.

Kind:      Type
Role:      the surfaced record
Projects:  finding

#### Binding Duplicate

The one token three surfaces are made to agree on — [Read Invocation]'s two fields, [Reconcile] step 5's scan, and Generation acceptance check 2 — and it answers **two** keys. On the **first key** it is two closing records naming one `invocation_id` that neither supersede one another nor are superseded, and it holds under every binding. On the **second key** it is two intents open at once on one `(kind, act_key)`, and it holds **only if** the act kind declares a `service_identity`: under `service_identity = none` several open intents are that deployment's declared degradation. §*Which closing stands* owns both keys, the supersession rule that exempts a correction, and the report-only branch; no surface restates them.

Kind:      Type
Role:      the conformance finding two writers leave
Projects:  binding_duplicate

#### Journal Unavailable

[Open]'s refusal where the pre-check's journal read failed, and [Reconcile]'s where its enumeration did: the journal could not be read, nothing was written, and the call may be retried. It is a read's failure and never a write's — a write that did not land is `recording-failure` at its position — and it is the one code this composition returns for an outage on the read path, at both positions that have one. [Close], [Refuse] and [Resolve] do not carry it: by the time they read, the act's existence is already decided, so their read failures land on their own position's `recording-failure` arm. [Read Invocation] **does** carry it, and is the third position: it decides nothing and has no `recording-failure` arm, so an outage there would otherwise be answered as absence.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the open rejections and the sweep's
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  journal_unavailable

#### Already Accounted

[Refuse]'s refusal where a closing record already names this `invocation_id` — the sweep or an operator closed the act while the adopter was refusing it. Carries the closing record's `event_id`. Nothing is appended, the act is accounted for, and the caller reports it rather than re-running: the mirror of [Close] adopting a landed outcome. Distinct from `not-open`, which is step 0's programming-error diagnosis and is never decided from a journal read — and deliberately not named `already-closed`, which is a **constituent's** own pre-commit code that an adopter transcribes verbatim two paragraphs from here (*Action wiring*), and which would put a store's "you cannot act on this" and this composition's "another writer already accounted for it" on one token.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the refuse rejections and the resolve rejections
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  already_accounted

#### Act Landed

[Open]'s refusal for a non-repeatable act whose outcome already exists; carries the `outcome_event_id`. The adopter's own re-entry arm answers the caller.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the open rejections
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  act_landed

## Decisions

- **2026-09-10 — Lease is the atom, and what a fence costs stays on this page.** *Chose:* discharge the standing extraction item with [`atoms/lease.md`](../atoms/lease.md) covering all three instances it required — the section's lease, `commit_fence` and `journal_fence` — as one concept, and keep on this page only the binding (which key, which holder, how long, which arm maps to which code) and §*Where the allowance goes*. *Over:* an atom covering the section alone, with the two fences left as prose. *Because:* the three are one concept — an instant after which a named holder's write cannot land, minted on one clock and judged on another — and an atom that took the section and left the fences would have left the page owning the same rule twice. The atom specifies the instant; **what carrying one costs a protocol is the protocol's, and the atom declines it**, which is why the three payments (a shorter usable lease, a widened abandon edge, a disclosed write bound with headroom) are stated here and not there. The measured result: lease-owned findings went 3 → 1 → **0** across the two gates either side of the extraction.

- **2026-09-10 — One owner for the conditions of instance start, and there are five of them.** *Chose:* a §*Instance start* section stating all five conditions, the arguments for each, and nothing else, with Configuration, Invariant 4, Generation acceptance check 4 and the walkthrough citing them by number. *Over:* the four-way statement the page carried for two rounds. *Because:* the four sites drifted in three separate directions at once — the walkthrough's own numbers breached a condition Configuration declared eight lines above them; the acceptance check that *is* instance start named four of the five; and the fifth condition's floor charged the shorter of the two leases a run may wait out. The `max` form matters and the enumeration says so: re-run over the same 432 tuples, the old floor admits 324 and the window inequality breaches on 15 of them, while the new one admits 288 and breaches on none. **This is the first round in five in which a fresh reader's prescribed remedy was confirmed by the evidence rather than refuted by it** — and the reason is visible: the enumerator had been measuring the `max` form since the round that wrote it, so what the reader found was the prose disagreeing with a check that already existed.

- **2026-09-10 — One owner for where the cross-seam allowance is spent.** *Chose:* a §*Where the allowance goes* section that classifies every cross-seam comparison as *applied* or *minted*, lists the three minted instants as cards, and owns the three payments a minted instant incurs. *Over:* leaving the disposition in *Bindings*, Configuration and *Primitive policies*. *Because:* those three had drifted to the point where one of them asserted, of another, the opposite of what that one said — Configuration told the reader that `commit_fence` carried a bare `expires_at` "which is where *Bindings* puts it", while *Bindings* minted the allowance in. Two generators reading the page produced two different stores' deadlines. **The gate's remedy was right about the defect and wrong about which passage was true**: the bare instant is the construction the Lease atom declares non-conforming, so the resolution is to mint and then to *size for the minting* — condition 3 of instance start charges the allowance wherever either fence is declared, which is what makes the gate's spurious-refusal scenario unreachable.

- **2026-09-10 — The findings register is extraction-pending, not a repudiation of the classification scheme.** *Chose:* classify `findings` as *extraction-pending* against a forthcoming **Condition Register**, restate the two obligations that attach, and name the five conditions no surface may read past. *Over:* the previous round's sentence declaring that the totality claim was simply false. *Because:* naming a truth-bearing composition element without classifying it is the unflagged store the execution contract calls a conformance finding; the scheme already has the case, and Audit Trail discharges it three times over for `erasure_outcomes` and the destruction record's two facts. An implementer reading *no classification* holds the register in process memory and loses exactly the outage span the page then asks an auditor to clear a check on.

- **2026-09-10 — The second key branches on `service_identity`; the first does not.** *Chose:* state the report-only branch once, at §*Which closing stands*, and have check 2, [Reconcile] step 5 and [Read Invocation] read it there. *Over:* an unbranched second key, which is what the page carried for two rounds. *Because:* [Open] step 3 *instructs* a report-only deployment to leave an old intent open and write a second one, so an unbranched key made this page's own instruction a conformance failure for a whole retention period — and made its reports indistinguishable from the fenceless-deployment duplicate the second key was added to catch, which destroys the signal. The first key is not branched because no report-only instruction on this page produces two closings.

- **2026-09-10 — `not-open` is a precondition on the caller, and the page says so rather than inventing a mechanism.** *Chose:* declare it the adopter's check, name the ephemeral local state that answers it (the pair [Open] returned, on the action's own call stack), and name what happens when a caller does not enforce it. *Over:* the gate's two prescriptions — a retained per-invocation handle, or deleting the code. *Because:* the handle is the composition-owned state the execution contract forbids, and deleting the code takes away the one name an adopter has for the condition. Both journal-derived sources stay foreclosed for one reason: on a multi-node instance a local absence is not a miss, so either would answer `not-open` for a live invocation. The cost — a bogus id reads as a yielded one and the caller is told not to re-run an act that never existed — is a false positive in the safe direction, and it is now written down instead of discovered.

- **2026-09-10 — The fence earns Invariant 8; the lease gate does not.** *Chose:* state the obligation directly — an intent is never in flight while its own invocation no longer holds the section — and close the finding at the **duplicate surfaces**, which now count open intents per `(kind, act_key)` as well as closings per `invocation_id`. *Over:* the remedy the gate prescribed, which was to gate [Open]'s intent write like every other write. *Because:* the model separates them and the prescription is aimed elsewhere — with no `journal_fence` the **gated** configuration still admits a second live intent, and with a fence the **ungated** one does not. The gate is added anyway, being this page's rule for every other write, but a deployment that adds it and reads it as the remedy has fixed nothing. The read it follows gains a disclosed bound (`read_bound`) for the same reason: three actions were spending an unbounded read inside a lease.

- **2026-09-10 — The sweep's lease is stated, not maximised.** *Chose:* `closure_latency + journal_write_bound`. *Over:* `max(completion_bound, closure_latency)` with a start check requiring it to exceed `closure_latency + journal_write_bound`. *Because:* that check reduces to `0 > journal_write_bound` whenever `closure_latency` reaches `completion_bound`, so every instance able to start had `completion_bound > closure_latency`, the `max` was identically `completion_bound`, and the case it existed to protect — a closure slower than the act's own bound, the ordinary shape of a cross-region probe — could not be configured at all. A lease stated directly is always satisfiable and needs no check. The schedule enumeration was re-run under the new definition before the prose was written: the window inequality holds on every tuple meeting `run_bound` at least `2 × closure_latency + journal_write_bound`, with the same one tick of slack.

- **2026-09-10 — Supersession is transitive, and the page says the model does not confirm it.** *Chose:* the standing closing is the one no other closing names, and everything reachable through `supersedes` counts with it as one, at all three surfaces. *Over:* the pairwise statement all three carried, which a chain breaks by putting its middle record in two pairs. *Because:* the chain is reachable — a sweep's escalation, an operator's abandonment, an operator's correcting outcome, which is the page's own check-6 path — and pairwise collapse reports `binding_duplicate` on it. **The model does not settle this and the Ledger says so:** it counts supersessions without identifying them, so a chain and two records naming one predecessor are the same state to it.

- **2026-09-08 — [Resolve] is modelled as the third writer, and two of its findings are settled by the model rather than by argument.** *Chose:* model v4, carrying the operator as a process that takes the same section on its own seam reading, with `SupersedesNamed` and `OperatorSkew` as constants so that the twin with each turned off is the page as written; and `service_identity` as a modelled dimension, which is what separates the operator's two arms. *Over:* repairing F6 and F7 from the gate's prescription, as three earlier rounds did — two of which prescribed a remedy that did not work. *Because:* the NOT MODELED list had named [Resolve] for three gates and the ninth returned five of seven foundational findings on it, which is a prediction paying out often enough to act on. The cost was one wrong turn worth recording: the operator's open-intent arm fired wherever an intent was past the edge, the state space did not terminate, and the fix was not a budget but a fidelity correction — an operator races the sweep to an open intent only in a deployment where the sweep writes nothing.

- **2026-09-08 — Supersession is always by name, and the operator's seam is the sixth clock.** *Chose:* every [Resolve] closing written over an existing record carries `supersedes` naming it, and `resolved_by` is not a supersession trigger; `now` is injected at the operator's seam alongside `operator_run_id`. *Over:* requiring the name for the abandoned case only, which is what the page said, and leaving [Resolve]'s too-young guard to a reading no seam supplies. *Because:* `resolved_by` names the operator and not a record, so the precedence rule and the acceptance check had no referent and both read a lawful resolution as a duplicate; and a guard that stops a destructive write cannot be decided by a reading nothing bounds. Both are rejected twins, each against a sibling that differs by exactly the one constant.

- **2026-09-08 — `kind` is an argument, and the act's closings are a declared index.** *Chose:* `kind` as the first parameter of every action but [Reconcile], `open_invocations` keyed on `(kind, act_key)`, and a fourth derived index `act_closings`. *Over:* leaving the kind implicit in the caller and letting three surfaces read records from a delta read that begins above them. *Because:* the page's own Configuration entry says a signature that omits the kind names the wrong one, and then omitted it from all seven of its own; and an exported code with a payload — `act-landed(outcome_event_id)` — had no state element that could supply the payload, so the guard never fired.

- **2026-08-30 — The closure window is bounded by three sweep runs, not by one run and a lease.** *Chose:* `completion_bound + 2 × clock_skew_allowance + 2 × reconciliation_cadence + 3 × run_bound < compensation_window`, with the sweep's lease removed as a term and a headroom obligation (`run_bound ≥ 2 × closure_latency + journal_write_bound`) put in its place. *Over:* the single-`run_bound` form this page carried for two rounds, and over the two-term correction the gate that found the defect prescribed. *Because:* a budgeted death involves three runs — the one in flight when the act crossed the examine edge, which may lawfully have missed it and then runs its own pass out; the one that dies; and the one that closes — and an enumeration of the schedules this page's own timeline admits breaches the old form on 420 of 432 parameter tuples and the prescribed correction on 396. The lease is not charged separately because `run_bound`'s declared "plus the waits" already carries one holder's remaining lease per act, which is sound only under the headroom obligation — and the tuples on which the new form breaches are exactly those that violate it, which is how the obligation was found rather than assumed.

- **2026-08-30 — A duplicate is decided by supersession first and position second.** *Chose:* a declared `binding_duplicate` field on [Read Invocation], a precedence rule that lets a `supersedes`/`resolved_by` record replace the one it names before position is consulted, a Generation acceptance check quantified over closing records of all four kinds and over intents, and a duplicate scan in [Reconcile] step 5 over the run's own delta. *Over:* the three detectors the fenceless degradation named in an earlier round, none of which existed: a surfacing clause in a step that only ever sees *open* intents, an acceptance check quantified over outcomes alone, and a field absent from the read's signature. *Because:* the one guarantee a real deployment in this library actually gets is the degraded one, and a degraded guarantee whose detection is asserted rather than specified is worth less than an honest statement that there is none. The scan rides the delta rather than the horizon because a duplicate is made by a record *landing*, and the substrate stamps `recorded_at` when it lands.

- **2026-08-30 — Fenceless is a declared degraded mode, not a refusal to start.** *Chose:* `journal_fence` optional, like `commit_fence`, with the weaker guarantee stated as something an auditor can check — at most one closing by a writer not paused across its terminus, and a `binding_duplicate` detected, resolved by lowest `sequence_number`, and surfaced where one is not. *Over:* requiring the fence, which no substrate in this library can supply, so that the composition would be unadoptable; and over the earlier phrasing, which quantified the degraded invariant over writes whose issuers were not paused — a set no auditor can enumerate. *Because:* this library already degrades honestly at `commit_fence = none` and `service_identity = none`, and the rule there is that the guarantee changes and stays checkable, not that the page stops.

- **2026-08-30 — The composition's own writes are fenced, not merely gated.** *Chose:* a declared `journal_fence` instance capability requirement, with Invariant 2 explicitly degraded where the substrate cannot offer one. *Over:* the `remaining` at least `journal_write_bound` gate alone, which this page relied on for two rounds. *Because:* the gate is check-then-act, and the page had already said so about relative timeouts under `commit_fence` — a paused holder passes the gate and writes afterwards. The formal model settles which mechanism carries the invariant: with the fence, Invariant 2 holds with or without the gate; without the fence, it fails with or without the gate.

- **2026-08-30 — The model's two corrections.** *Chose:* a section host that releases only on return or expiry — a holder's death is not an event it sees — and a closure inequality that budgets one dead run (`2 × completion_bound`). *Over:* release on the holder's death, and the inequality with one bound, as the draft first had them. *Because:* the formal model's death twin lands two closings for one act — a run dies with its write in flight, the section frees, the other node re-reads before the write lands — and its window probes breach at `completion_bound + reconciliation_cadence + run_bound` once a run may die, and hold one lease later.

Directional changes only — the turns a future reader must know the pattern took, and why. Everything smaller lives in the commit that made it: `git log -- compositions/recoverable-invocation.md`.

- **2026-08-30 — The protocol is a compound, adopted by binding.** *Chose:* one composition owning the intent/outcome pair, the act's section with lease-as-terminus semantics, the two-edged sweep under a service identity behind a recovery-intended record, the derived-not-remembered rule with candidates, positioned codes, and the closure inequality — with adopters supplying `act_key`, `commit`, `probe`, `completion_bound`, `journal`, `service_identity`, `outcome_envelope`, and `retry_terminus`, and declaring deviations from a closed list. *Over:* six rules on the methodology page applied twenty times, or a Transaction pattern the constituents cannot support. *Because:* the methodology's higher-order composition test — fourteen exact instances, roughly 920 kilobytes of protocol prose, and closure checks finding the identical residue (an undeclared section, unstated lease semantics) in every copy — and because the protocol's invariants are provable only over the pair of processes and the one section, which is the same pair in every adopter and belongs to none of their acts.
