F1 — The `journal_fence = none` degradation names three detectors, and none of the three exists as described [structural]
  WHERE: *Composes* §Instance capability requirements, the `journal_fence = none` paragraph; [Reconcile] step 2; Generation acceptance check 2; [Read Invocation] signature; Invariant 2.
  DEFECT: Composes concedes that without `journal_fence` — "the case in this library today" — Invariant 2 degrades to *duplicates are detected and resolved rather than prevented*, and names exactly three mechanisms that make the degraded form checkable. All three fail. (a) "[Reconcile] step 2 surfaces any act whose re-read finds more than one closing" — but step 2 iterates only over the intents step 1 kept, and step 1 keeps only *open* intents (`open_invocations` is the `<kind>.intended` records "less those a later record names through `invocation_id`"). An act with two closings is named by both, so it is not open, is never enumerated, and never reaches step 2; step 2's own text contains no surfacing clause at all, only "release, next". (b) "Generation acceptance check 2 reads a second closing as this conformance failure" — check 2's own text says "at most one **outcome** record names its `invocation_id`", the exact outcome-only quantifier Invariant 2 says it abandoned because it "let an escalation and an outcome coexist in a shape no section of it defined". (c) "[Read Invocation] … marks the entry `binding-duplicate = true`, and returns every duplicate in `records`" — [Read Invocation]'s signature block declares `{invocation_id, state, records, more}` and no `binding-duplicate` field, and its prose never mentions duplicates. So the one guarantee a real deployment actually gets is asserted to be checkable and is not.
  FAILURE: `journal_fence = none`. Invocation I passes [Close] step 2's re-read, is paused, and wakes after sweep run N wrote `<kind>.escalated` for I's `invocation_id`; I appends its outcome. Now one intent carries an escalation and an outcome. Run N+1's step 1 drops the act (it is closed), so step 2 never sees it. Check 2 counts outcomes: one — pass. Check 3 wants *a* closing within the window: two exist — pass. [Read Invocation] returns a single `state` with no duplicate flag. The `binding-duplicate` the page promises is detected by nobody.
  FIX: Widen check 2's quantifier to *closing* records of all four kinds; give [Read Invocation] a declared `binding-duplicate` field with the lowest-`sequence_number` selection rule and the full duplicate list; and add the duplicate scan to [Reconcile] as a pass over *closed* intents inside the horizon (step 1 keeps only open ones, so it cannot live in step 2).

F2 — The window inequality under-budgets a dead sweep run by one whole `run_bound` [timing]
  WHERE: Configuration §`compensation_window`; Invariant 4 liveness arm; Generation acceptance check 4; [Reconcile] step 5's derived threshold.
  DEFECT: The inequality carries exactly one `run_bound` and charges a dead run only `max(completion_bound, closure_latency)` — "a run that dies holding an act's section holds it until *its own* lease expires, and the next run's take waits exactly that long." That is the only cost the page attributes to a death. But `run_bound` is defined per run — "from a [Reconcile] run's start to the last closing it lands" — and a death necessarily involves two runs. The dead run's own elapsed pass, up to a full `run_bound` during which it did not reach this act, is charged to nothing. The narration ("the next run starts at most a cadence later; a run that dies costs one more lease; the last closing lands `run_bound` after that") is only true of a run that dies at the instant it starts. Whenever `run_bound > max(completion_bound, closure_latency)` — the normal case, since `run_bound` is the backlog times `closure_latency` while the lease is one act's bound — a deployment can clear the start check and still breach the window on the one case the window claims to budget. The walkthrough survives on slack (362 s of worst case against 600 s), not because the inequality is sound.
  FAILURE: `completion_bound = 30 s`, `closure_latency = 5 s`, `clock_skew_allowance = 2 s`, `reconciliation_cadence = 60 s`, `run_bound = 400 s` (80 sequential closures), `compensation_window = 530 s`, `retention_period = 700 s`. Start check A: 30 + 30 + 4 + 60 + 400 = 524 < 530 ✓. Start check B: 30 + 2 + 530 = 562 < 700 ✓ — the instance starts. Orphan intent at `t`, examinable from `t + 32`. A run starts at `t + 92`, works its backlog, and dies at `t + 480` without having reached this act (so the lease term the inequality budgeted is never even spent). The restart run begins at `t + 480`; its disclosed bound is 400 s, so its last closing may land at `t + 880` — 350 s past the window Invariant 4 promises and check 4 certified, with no second death, no outage, and no fence involved.
  FIX: Carry two `run_bound` terms when a death is budgeted — `completion_bound + max(completion_bound, closure_latency) + 2 × clock_skew_allowance + reconciliation_cadence + 2 × run_bound < compensation_window` — and rederive [Reconcile] step 5's threshold from the corrected form.

  FIX: Give [Open], [Close], [Refuse], and [Resolve] a declared arm for a failed pre-check read — `rejected(recording-failure(intent))` at [Open] and the corresponding position elsewhere, nothing written, section released — and restate Invariant 4's outage clause as *the intent stays open and [Open] refuses*, naming that arm rather than `act-in-flight`.

F4 — [Resolve] tells the operator to retry a journal failure the position rule says left the record appended [semantic]
  WHERE: [Resolve], "A journal failure on its closing write lands `recording-failure(resolution)` — the position is nominal: nothing about the act changed, the operator retries"; Primitive policies, the position rule's outcome position; Terms §Recording Failure.
  DEFECT: The position rule is explicit that at a post-commit position `recording-failure(step-4)` and the retention-source `invalid-request` mean the record **is** appended — "read its `event_id` back by `invocation_id` and return success with a hard alert" — and it says of the intent position "never retry (a retry appends a second intent)". [Open], [Close], [Refuse], and [Reconcile] each transcribe that arm. [Resolve] alone does not: it collapses every journal failure into `recording-failure(resolution)`, asserts "nothing about the act changed", and directs a retry. On the `step-4` arm the closing record is already in the journal, so the retry appends a second closing for one `invocation_id`. This produces the duplicate Invariant 2 forbids in a deployment that *has* `journal_fence` — no pause, no lease straddle, no degradation involved. The Terms card repeats the wrong reading ("`resolution` means an operator's closing record did not land and nothing changed"), so two sections agree on it.
  FAILURE: An operator resolves an escalated act. `record_action` returns `recording-failure(step-4)`: the `<kind>.disclosed` closing is appended and attested, only its retention placement failed. Per [Resolve] the operator receives `recording-failure(resolution)` and retries. A second closing lands. Check 2 now reads two outcomes for one `invocation_id` — `binding-duplicate` — and Invariant 2 is broken by the page's own instruction.
  FIX: Transcribe the outcome position in [Resolve]: `step-4` and the retention-source `invalid-request` → read the closing back by `invocation_id`, return success with a hard alert, never retry; reserve `recording-failure(resolution)` for `step-2 | step-3` and a lost reply after the read-back, and correct the Terms card.

F5 — [Resolve] lands two codes its signature does not declare, one of them the payload the prose calls load-bearing [structural]
  WHERE: [Resolve]'s signature block versus its prose.
  DEFECT: The signature is `rejected(not-open | section-unavailable | invalid-credential | invalid-request | recording-failure(resolution))`. The prose lands two codes that are not in it: `rejected(not-known)` for "an `invocation_id` with no readable intent record at all", and `rejected(invalid-request(purged))` for an intent the substrate reports *Purged*. The signature carries only bare `invalid-request`. The page argues at length that both distinctions are semantically load-bearing — `not-known` is "deliberately *not* `not-open`" because "reusing it here … would put two unrelated diagnoses on one code", and the `(purged)` cause is carried "since an operator must tell lawful destruction, where there is nothing to do, from a malformed disposition, which they can fix and retry". A generator building [Resolve] from its signature emits neither, and an operator receiving bare `invalid-request` cannot make the very distinction the prose says they must.
  FAILURE: An operator calls [Resolve] on an intent whose payload the substrate purged. The implementation, built to the signature, returns `rejected(invalid-request)`. The operator reads it as a malformed disposition, corrects the disposition, and retries — repeatedly, against a record that no longer exists and is owed no closing (Invariant 7).
  FIX: Write the landed codes into the block: `rejected(not-open | not-known | section-unavailable | invalid-credential | invalid-request(purged | malformed) | recording-failure(resolution))`.
