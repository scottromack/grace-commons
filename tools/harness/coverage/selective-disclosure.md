## Step 3 — bound saturation

Alloy reports no state count, so the scope is raised once and the verdict compared:

- At `for 4` (the shipped bound): all 21 checks UNSAT, all 6 runs SAT.
- At `for 5` on a scratch copy: the same verdicts → **saturated** ☑ (about 20 s). Four calls already give two records of one subject, one field set recorded twice, a backdated and a defaulted instant beside refused calls; a fifth adds a larger state and no new shape of a record.

TLA+: two disclosure ids and room for two records, two subject references, one recipient, scope and authority reference, the three authority types, the clock from 0 to 1. At those bounds a record is written backdated and at now, two records share a subject, a field set and an instant, a later record carries a lower id, a refused call follows a recorded one, and every well-formed query (288 of them) is asked in every state.

- At the shipped bound: 1,194 distinct states.
- Raised once on a scratch copy, the clock to 2: 3,249 distinct states, every check holds → the same verdicts (about 23 s). The extra instant adds states (a clock always does) and no shape a check reads: a backdated record, a shared instant and a later record with a lower id are already reached at 1.
