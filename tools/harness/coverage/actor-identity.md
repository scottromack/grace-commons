## Step 3 — bound saturation

Alloy reports no state count, so the scope is raised once and the verdict compared:

- At `for 4` (the shipped bound): all 21 checks UNSAT, all 4 runs SAT.
- At `for 5` on a scratch copy: the same verdicts → **saturated** ☑ (about 14 s). Four calls already give two admitted attestations of one action at one instant beside refused calls of every kind; a fifth adds a longer history and no new shape of a record.

TLA+: one action and one actor reference, three ids, room for two attestations, two keys, the clock from 0 to 1. At those bounds an attestation is recorded under either key, the registry answers material, unknown-actor and unreachable, rotates keeping or dropping the old key, a revocation arrives, a second attestation of the same action, actor and instant is recorded, and every [Verify] answer is reached on a recorded attestation (verified under historical material included); an id names nothing.

- At the shipped bound: 5,568 distinct states (about 30 s on one worker).
- Raised once on a scratch copy, the clock to 2: 13,392 distinct states, every check holds → the same verdicts (43 s on 8 workers). The extra instant adds states (a clock always does) and no shape a check reads. Two larger raises, three attestations and four ids, and two action references with the clock to 2, were stopped at 280 s on one worker (about 29,000 and 34,000 states, no violation by then) and are not claimed.
