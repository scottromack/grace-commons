## Step 3 — bound saturation

Model bound: `MaxP` preference-record slots for one principal. At `MaxP=3` (implied by typical config): sufficient to exercise SetFresh, Suspend, SetSupersede (supersedes Active), SetSupersede (supersedes Suspended), Delete on Active, Delete on Suspended. Appears adequate; the exclusivity claim only requires observing the two-in-effect window, which needs at least two slots.

Alloy (2026-11-11): `message-preference.als` checks at `for 4 but 4 Int` (four calls build a version three calls deep, set → suspend → delete, or a supersession beside a [Current For] or [Read] over both records, or two [Current For] calls over one database; four-bit integers hold every instant, only compared). Raised once to `for 5 but 4 Int` on a scratch copy: the same verdicts, 22 checks UNSAT and 10 runs SAT. A copy without `HostSuppliesEachIdOnce` breaks `A_Instance_2` alone: the page promises no reuse (Identity 4, Instance 2) but the id is the host's (Identity 2), and its Capability requirement 9 (an id no record has carried) is the weaker reading Logic confinement 16 names, under which two calls in flight may share one; only Logic confinement 16, which the page does not cite, makes the id fresh. A copy without `ClockNeverGoesBackward` breaks `A_Temporal_property_1`, `_2` and `_3` alone: no guard of the page orders the instants, so they hold only on the deployment's monotonic clock (Logic confinement 7).
